# Felix

Felix is the app's voice: the persona Claude writes as, in the chat and in the messages the app sends about your work. A strict friend who knows your week; Claude is the model behind him.
He can change almost anything about your work, each change made as he gives it with an Undo, and he never touches the blocker, protection or Claude's own settings.
Everything each of his calls is sent, circumstance by circumstance and with examples, is in [felix-inputs.md](felix-inputs.md).
His code is in `felix/`: `Conversation` (the chat, a reply to his notification, what he remembers, the week's check-in, what the app says in his name), `Looks` (what he writes unprompted) and `Outbox` (every message in his name into the chat first, and his one notification made from it), each built like `Focus`: its stores, the clock, and `FelixPorts` for what Android does. `ConversationTest`, `LooksTest` and `OutboxTest` drive them on temporary stores, his calls answered by a stand-in for Claude's API (`FelixHarness`).
The chat is his one visible voice (D-95): every word in his name is a message there, and a notification of his is made from it, never beside it.

## Who he is

- His name and the line that says who he is are settings (`felixName`, `felixLine`, by default "your mate who's on your case about the work"), changed in Settings → Felix or by him; every prompt of his names him with them (AI-22).
- How he writes (D-57): like a sixth-former texting a mate, set by his two prompts' one register paragraph (`FelixPrompts.REGISTER`) and pinned by each prompt's made-up examples (AI-07): lower case, contractions, the odd "alr" or "tbh", short and light on commas; exact where it matters; no emojis. A message that's just talk gets a reply and nothing else (`FelixPrompts.JUST_TALK`). The app's own lines in his name (`FelixWords`) are in the same register, and his chips too, the app taking either their words or its own.
- Settings → Felix also has how far into a slot he asks (`felixGraceMin`, 10 minutes), when he checks again if he doesn't say (`felixDefaultCheckMin`, 20), and whether he may search the web (`felixSearches`, on).
- A message of his is `Speaker.Felix` (from 4.0.1), stored as "Claude", as it was before; the screens show his name.
- His button is on every screen: Day, Work, Stats, Settings, a task's sheet (*Ask Felix about it*), and the block screen, about the next piece's task (UI-16). It wears the count of his messages unread (`Conversation.unread`): those since you last left the chat, but a question you've answered, or a message you've replied to, from his notification (`Outbox.repliedTo`; only that one: what the notification left out stays unread, 4.1.2).
- While Claude is off (`ModelGateway.claudeKey` null) he writes nothing unprompted of his own, and the chat says why your message waits; the app's own words still go in his name: the morning's list, how work went, the check-in's and a calendar event's questions, and your reminders, in your words.

## The chat

`ui/AskActivity.kt` has two tabs, *Chat* and *Your changes*; its menu has *Mark all answered*, *What Felix remembers* and *Clear the chat*. The chat is on screen only on its tab: *Your changes* shown, it's left as on leaving the screen, and back, opened again; every way into the screen, his notification's tap among them, shows the *Chat* tab. The chat holds every message of his, what he writes unasked among them, and every one is kept (D-96):
- the live window in `chat.json`: the newest 200 messages or those of the last 14 days, whichever keeps more (D-96), and every one of his still unread; what's past it and read moves to the archive, a file a month (`chat-archive-<yyyy-MM>.json`, `ChatArchiveStore`), oldest first, the archive written before the window is trimmed (`Outbox.moveWindow`);
- clearing the chat empties both, and leaves what it made and his state;
- it reads as a messaging app's (`ui/ChatScreenState.kt`): each day's date over its first message ("Today", "Yesterday", "Tuesday 13 October"), the time under each, and a *New messages* line over the first of his messages unread when it was opened, for that visit, one you've replied to from his notification not among them, and his notification's tap opening it at the first you haven't (4.1.2);
- it opens at that message, or, from his notification, at the message the notification was for, tinted for a moment, else at its foot; leaving it, what's in it is read (`ChatState.readAt`, `Conversation.read`);
- scrolled back to its top, the archive's messages show, a page of 100 at a time (*Earlier messages*), never put back in the window: its newest months read as far as the pages go, off the main thread, and none at all till you scroll back (whether it holds any is said by whether it has a month's file); a reply on screen quoting one of its messages has that message read for its quote, and a quote tapped further back reads the months back to it; They're shown with nothing to do on them (their cards' and lines' Undo are the window's), and a reply to one is routed and quoted as any is (`Outbox.find`), a question answered from there marked answered there.
- a message, his or yours, swiped right past a third of the width (with a haptic tick) or held for *Reply* is the one your next replies to: a strip over the box says whose and its first line, with ✕ to reply to none. Sent, yours carries it (`replyTo`) and shows it over your words, tinted, where it isn't the message just above; tapped, the chat goes to it, from the archive too. Held, *Copy* copies a message's words.
- when a question of his still waits for its answer since your last turn of the chat (the check-in's under way, the one it asked last, never one from a week gone; a reading's, how work went, a calendar event's: `Replies.pending`), the box and his chips answer it, its strip over the box by itself, and its ✕ sends your words to him instead (D-98); with several asked together (how work went, a calendar's questions), the one before is next once one's answered. His newest message still waiting since your last turn (below), a nudge as much as a question, takes the box and the chips: a nudge just above the box with no strip, your words its reply (`replyTo`) taking none of its subject, so they're told him as they are (`Conversation.ask`'s `takeItsSubject`), one further up with its strip; an older question waits, shown so, and is the box's again once the nudge is marked answered (4.0.10).
- his notification holds his unread messages, as many as fit about 360 characters, the newest first, each ended at a whole sentence where it runs past that (`Outbox.NOTICE_ROOM`): One UI holds an expanded notification's height and cuts off what runs past it, the Reply and chips with it; the older ones are in the chat, which a tap opens at the first unread you haven't replied to;
- his messages waiting for your answer say so under them, "waiting for your answer" by the time (D-99, `Replies.awaitingIds`): his questions above till they're answered or a message of yours replies to them (a calendar event's only till the event begins), and his nudges with replies of their own, each till you reply to it, a newer message of his is about the same task (or, about none, of the same kind), or its day is over; tapped, that line is the message's *Reply*. A reply changes nothing of his in the store: a message of his waits no more once one of yours replies to it (`ChatMessage.replyTo`), so his notification still shows your words under it while he answers. Held, *Mark answered* lets one go, and the menu's *Mark all answered* every one, with an Undo for six seconds (`Conversation.markAnswered`: answered, so out of his notification too); a check-in's question while the check-in's under way isn't let go. He marks his nudges too (4.2.2): the chat's context lists those still waiting by id (the newest ten), and where your words answer one without replying to it, his answer names it (`answered`), marked as *Mark answered* marks it, at his word, and said under his answer ("Taken as answered: …", with no Undo); an id he names that wasn't listed is dropped. A question whose answer the app keeps (a reading's, how work went, a calendar event's, the check-in's) and a reminder aren't listed: each waits for its reply, which records its answer.
- under an answer of his, what he did is folded (`ChatSummary`): his words alone, and a chevron at the bubble's corner, red where something went wrong (not made, not looked up, not brought, left out). Open (the chevron, or a tap on his bubble), his cards, acts, what he remembered, his searches and what was left out show as they always did, with every Undo; leaving the chat closes every one. A task he shows you, his chips and a button in the app's words stay outside the fold. A message of his that arrives while the chat is on screen, or was unread when it opened, has a label slide out from behind its bubble for five seconds ("Changes: 2 · Remembered · Searched", what went wrong in red), once; tapped, it opens the fold. A message that's just talk has neither.
- You write by keyboard or voice (UI-07). A message from a task's, an event's or a day's page carries what it's about (`About`).
- A message sent while he answers another of yours lets that answer go, paid for and unused, with nothing more looked up or read for it, and he answers both together (D-98); what waits, waits together, the newest saying why (taken back, the one before it says it), and what you sent waiting is sent to him with it, a reply to an older nudge too; one past the conversation's last 12 says it was left out, and waits for its *Try again*. While Claude can't be asked (off, resting, at its cap) yours says why, and it's answered once Claude can be: the chat opened again, *Try again*, Claude back, or after a sync.
- While he answers, three dots show; after 10 seconds *Cancel* lets the answer go (AC-08). A call once sent is paid for, so it isn't closed.
- An answer the app can't use (declined, cut off, unreadable) waits for *Try again*, as each try is paid for.
- His suggested replies, up to three (`suggestedReplies`), are chips under his last message, the message the box answers, or one of his you chose to reply to (above) (UI-06); a tap is a reply to it, as one from anywhere is (below): a check-in's, a calendar question's or how work went with no call.
- A message in the app's words can carry a button of its own (`ChatMessage.opens`): the week's review to open, its summary to send.

`felix/ChatReader.kt` makes the chat's call (`ChatReader.answer`): one for a message, and one for each round of what he asks for (two at most, `ChatRounds.MAX_ROUNDS`), at medium effort (AC-02), under the cap (AC-05). Someone is waiting, so it goes through a rest after a failure, but not past a key problem or the cap (AC-04).
- `ChatPrompts.system`: who he is, what he can do (below), and how to answer: one to three short sentences saying what he's done, one short question and no change when he needs to know more, and nothing he isn't asked for.
- `ChatPrompts.turns`: what `ChatPrompts.history` takes of the chat (D-97): the conversation's last 12 messages (`HISTORY`: yours, and his answers to you) and his last two nudges (`NUDGES`: what he wrote unasked), each with your reply, in their order, starting with one of yours; each earlier answer with its cards and what became of them; a nudge before any of yours said in your first turn. A reply that went to no call (a check-in's answer) goes only with its nudge, so nudges can't push the conversation out. The message being answered is the newest of yours: what he wrote unasked after it isn't sent with it. A message of yours that replies to one that isn't just before it starts with that one, quoted from the whole chat ("(Replying to your message: “…”)"), and the prompt says that's what it answers; a message the app wrote in his name is marked "(the app wrote this for you)".
- All of it within 40 000 characters (`Pricing.MAX_INPUT_CHARS`): your newest message whole, earlier turns in up to a quarter of the rest, then the context, then the plan, which keeps at least a third of their room.
- `ChatAnswers.parse` checks his answer against what there is. What can't be used is left out and said under the reply ("Left out, as the app can't use it: …").

## What he's told

With your newest message, from one snapshot of the tasks, the calendar and your changes, which his answer is then checked against (`Conversation.chatContext`):
- `ChatPrompts.context`: today and the time; the open tasks (id, title, source, its class and the subject the class map gives it (4.1.1), kind, due date and whether it's a real deadline or a plan, minutes, minutes worked, can't start until), each with its steps by place;
- tasks that look like the same work (DX-15), the fortnight's calendar events, the changes in use, your own tasks finished in the last week, and the settings he may change, with their values;
- **each class's next lessons** (G8, 4.1.1, `core/Lessons.kt`): its next three, after the calendar's events, from Power Planner's timetable as last read (DX-09), looked for as far as the plan's reach so a holiday doesn't hide them, none on a day of no school nor from a half day's end; a Power Planner class and a Teams code with the same subject in Settings → About you's classes are one class (that list gains the timetable's classes), as is a Teams code given a Power Planner class's own name (the class it is, nothing mapped back), one with no subject tied to no code; dated where the timetable's over a week old, or said to be of no known age where its last read's time is lost, left out with none read;
- **your pace**: the multipliers the calibration has learned, by kind and class, with how many timed tasks each rests on, or that nothing's learned yet;
- what each task's minutes come to in the plan: "planned as 54 at their pace" for the app's estimates, "as given" for your minutes and for steps you or he gave, and a paced total as below;
- **each open task's own text** (D-93, `enrich/TaskText.kt`): an assignment's instructions, an email's conversation, a planner item's details, your notes, each at most 600 characters (`ChatPrompts.BRIEF_TEXT`, 12 000 in all), what's attached, its links, an email's sender and the whole text's length on its line in the list (`TaskText.holds`, 160 characters a line, 6 000 in all, past that its length alone); last in the context, so it's what a long one loses first. A longer text is its reading's summary of all of it (`Enrichment.summary`, [readings.md](readings.md)); past the 16 000 characters a reading sees (`ReadingPrompts.MAX_TEXT`), of its start, with its end after it; with none (Claude off when it was read, or read before summaries), its start and its end, saying how much of the middle was left out: a conversation is kept oldest first, so its end holds the newest replies;
- `ChatPrompts.plan`: the plan as Day shows it: the slot under way or the next thing to do; whether blocking applies and why, by the plan he's shown (a slot missed today with no time left today keeping it on till the day's hours end, BL-11), the free time and what finishing earns (G5, 4.1.1, `Focus.blockingLine`); today's slots each past, now or ahead (AI-13), with their keys; the next piece of each task with no steps, by key, and the pieces kept today (G1); what isn't planned and why, other events, and what's planned past the fortnight;
- then each of the plan's next 14 days, with its minutes against its working time and each piece's reason;
- the profile every call carries (AI-05, AI-06; [privacy.md](privacy.md)).

What your message is about comes with it in full, before your words (D-93): a task's whole text (`ChatPrompts.WHOLE_TEXT`, 8 000 characters; past that, its reading's summary, then its start and end) with your answers to its questions, or an event's place and description.

**Asking for more** (`details`): beside tasks' texts, the week's numbers (`stats`, G6: this week so far and last week, by subject, `Stats.weekLines`) and the calendar past the fortnight (`calendar`, the four weeks after it, or `calendar:YYYY-MM`, a month, up to 60 events, saying where the calendar's read ends: `ChatPrompts.calendarBlock`, G7), each once, said under his answer ("Read: the week's numbers."); his prompt's item 7 says what he can ask for and when (D-102). Two spans of the calendar a message at most (`ChatRounds.MAX_SPANS`); what's brought beside the texts has a room of its own, 12 000 characters, a block past its share cut at a whole line, saying how many lines were left out. The last round a message has is told it's the last; what he still asks for after it is said under his answer ("Not brought: …", in red), and a search that couldn't be made is told to the next round, so he doesn't ask for it again.

**Reading a task in full** (`details`, D-93): where he needs more of a task's text than the context gives, his answer names it (three at most, never the one the message is about, which came whole) and says only that he's reading it; he's asked again with their whole texts (each an even share of 16 000 characters, 8 000 at most) after your words, and that answer is his, with "Read in full: …" under it. Asked for a web search too, he's asked once with both; where the texts show a need for a search, or the search for a text, once more, never more (`ChatRounds`). The texts he read are kept till he's answered with them, so a failed answer is asked again with them, not for them again.

## What he can do

Everything he gives is made as his answer comes, each with its Undo (next section).
- **New tasks of your own** (`newTasks`, at most ten): title, kind (Homework, Revision, Admin or Event), subject, due date and time (with no time, an event's is 09:00, anything else's the end of the day), start, minutes, notes, and steps for a big one, each saying whether it needs the steps before it done first (`anyOrder`, D-109). Each is checked as the form checks one (`OwnTaskRules.problem`), its dates from a month back to just over a year ahead.
- **Changes** (`changes`, at most 40), of an instruction's types (`InstructionPrompts.CHANGE_TYPES`): a task isn't one, can't start until a date, waits for another, is due by a date, takes N minutes, goes on one day, has its next piece at a time, is another's work, has these steps or this name;
- and for days and events: how long an event takes, a day's limit, busy times, days off and half-days (into the school calendar, PL-16).
- **Steps** for any task but Anki's, whose steps are its cards (AI-19): laid over the source's and the reading's (`TaskItem.mine.steps`, set by him), ticks kept by id, each saying whether it needs the steps before it done first (`anyOrder`, D-109). They must add up to the task's minutes, or come with a Minutes change for the new total, or they're left out.
- **Memories** (`memories`): facts and standing instructions (below).
- **Actions** (`actions`, AI-14): tick or untick a step (its slot kept where the plan has one, earning as a tick does); finish, reopen or delete one of your own tasks (or feedback done by its tick, NF-06); open a task on a card, or where it lives (`open` with value `source`, G3: Teams, Power Planner, Gmail or AnkiDroid, as its sheet's Open does, at once, said under his answer, while the chat's on screen (left as it opens, its card's there too): else, from his notification's reply, a retry in the background or with the chat left, on a card, its sheet's Open opening it there); set a setting.
- **A piece kept, or taken back** (G1, 4.1.1): `keepPiece` keeps a piece that has no step to tick (a task in boxes, a slot of today's) as Day's Done keeps it (`Focus.keepSlot`), by a key the plan he's shown lists, as of that plan (one kept from Day while he answered isn't kept again, a box's next having its key); `unkeepPiece` takes back one kept today (`Focus.unkeepSlot`), one done ahead of its day too, the record he was shown (one kept again while he answered is yours), by a key from his context's *Kept today*; one that finished a task of yours (or one done by its tick) opens it again with it, as the keep's Undo does (one finished another way since stays so, and nothing's taken back); one not taken back for that, or as it was kept again while he answered, is said under his answer. Each has its Undo, the reverse: a keep's takes back the record it made, while it's its key's newest, a task of yours (or one done by its tick) it finished open again, as a tick's Undo does (one finished another way since stays so); an unkeep's keeps it again, as it was kept (its extra minutes, its photo, its source's word), while it isn't kept again since (an older box's record under its key is no bar), the day of the record it took back only (past midnight its key names another day's piece), and while its piece is laid as it was then (one laid otherwise since, its box's length or its task's minutes changed, is another: `Focus.pieceMinutes`).
- **A source read now** (`sync`, G2, 4.1.1): Teams, Power Planner, Gmail or Anki, through the Sync service (`Sync.syncNow`, a callback, as Sync calls the Conversation), said under his answer ("Asked Teams to read") with no Undo; Teams', with the chat on screen, asks the Teams widget to sync it too, as a tap on its refresh does (Q33: B, D-11: "Asked Teams to read, the Teams widget syncing it now"), and from his notification's reply, with the chat left or the widget refusing, reads what the widget has, the line saying why; when the read changes the task he was asked about, one of that source's (finished, open again, due another day, its words, by the source's own values, so a change made here meanwhile isn't said as the source's), within a quarter of an hour (a read after it says nothing), a note of his says so ("Teams says “Forces worksheet” is handed in."), the app's words; finished only as done (an Anki quota missed, taken over by homework, isn't studied).
- **A due date at a class's next lesson** (G8, 4.1.1): a DueBy with `lesson` the class (its subject, or one of its names), its own date and time not read: the app sets it to that class's next lesson's start; refused, saying why, with no timetable read, one over a week old or of no known age, or a class the lessons' list doesn't have, so he asks you the date.
- **Settings**, only those in `core/FelixSettings.kt`: his name and line, when he asks and checks, the briefings and the check-in, working hours, the morning break, the shortest school time used, pieces and their breaks, breaks outside, margins, undated work, the Anki deadline and textbook.
- **Reminders** (changes of type `Remind`, at most ten; 4.1.0, D-100): your words, up to 300 characters, at a time (today's with no date, from now to a year ahead), at the start of a task's next work slot, or a while before a task's deadline in force (a week before at most), following it as it moves (`core/Reminders.kt`). One already set, by its id from his context's list, is moved or reworded in place while it's never come; `cancelReminder` cancels one. At most 50 still to come. When one comes is in [His messages](#his-messages).
- **Notes** (changes of type `Note`, at most ten; 4.1.0, D-100): on a listed task, open or finished, ten a task at most; or standing (Q31: A), for every task of a subject (by the class map, or a subject it begins in whole words: "Physics" for a class given as "Physics HALE"), a class, a source or with a word in its title (from a word's start: "art" isn't in "Cartesian"), each named in at most 60 characters, twenty at most (`core/Notes.kt`, `Notes.forTask`); `deleteNote` deletes one. In the chat a task's own are told under its steps, in a room of their own, a standing one once, by its id under each task it matches, and a finished task's in a list of their own, the newest first, in what's left of that room, so he can say or delete them; in his messages about a task and to the micro-planner, both; each is shown on its tasks' sheets with its delete (a standing one's asks first: it goes from every task it's for), where you add your own (Q14: A). A note added or deleted makes the plan again, so its pieces' first steps are written with it. Kept in `felix.json`, by task id, so a sync can't drop one.
- Reminders and notes come as changes of their own types on the changes' fields, the chat's alone (Q13: B): lists of their own made the chat's grammar too large for the API to compile. A Remind's words are its title, when it comes its eventKey (`at`, `slotStart`, `beforeDue`), a listed one's id to move its afterTaskId and a BeforeDue's lead its minutes; a standing Note's scope is its eventKey as kind:value (`subject:Chemistry;source:Teams`). The chat's parse takes them out before the changes are read (`ChatAnswers.parse`), so the instructions screen's reader and its changes are as they were.

Never the blocker's settings, protection's or Claude's, the calendars, your classes, your profile, the Teams syncs, his web search or where the summary goes; a value the Settings screen wouldn't take is refused (`FelixSettings.set`).
A task from Teams, Power Planner or AnkiDroid is done when its source says so: he can't mark one done or delete it, only say it isn't a task. A starred email he can finish with `done`, with the switch on (D-106): it's unstarred and archived in Gmail a minute later, and closed at Gmail's read after; the very first waits for your OK in the app; his act's Undo takes back the finish it made, not a newer one, even with the email's task gone after a backup's restored; a `done` while one's being finished already is said so, with nothing of its own to undo. The context marks each email "finish with done". With the switch off, an email is done when Gmail says so, as a Teams task is: asked to finish one, he says he can't finish it in Gmail and asks whether to mark it not a task instead, never calling it done (`ChatPrompts.system`; `chat-gmail-off.txt` is its golden copy).

## Made at once, with an Undo

`ChatAnswers.cards` turns his answer into cards, each made as if applied (`Proposal.instant`, `Conversation.applyProposal`): his changes on one card, each new task on its own, and an *Open* card for each task he shows you.
- A changes card becomes an instruction in the words that asked for it, listed under *Your changes*. On your own tasks, their due dates, starts, minutes, steps and names are made on the task itself (`OwnTaskRules.MADE_ON_IT`), the rest laid over it as any instruction is; days off go into the school calendar.
- Each card says *Done* or *Added*, with *Undo* (`Conversation.undoProposal`); a task he added has *Change* (the form); one undone has *Redo*.
- One that couldn't be made says "Not made: …" under his answer; a time the plan couldn't keep says where it went: "06:35 had no free time: it's at 10:45." (`ChatAnswers.movedPins`, PL-04).
- His actions are listed under his answer (`ChatMessage.acts`, `Act` in `core/Chat.kt`), each with its *Undo* while `ChatActs.canUndo` finds it as he left it (`Conversation.undoAct`).
- Reminders and notes are acts too (`Conversation.keep`): "Reminder set: Tue 20 Oct 19:00, “call gran”", "Moved: “call gran” to …" (its old time and words kept on the act), "Cancelled: the reminder …", "Noted on “…”: …", "Note deleted: …". A cancel and a delete only mark what they name, kept whole for the Undo. A reminder set or moved can be taken back while it's never come nor been snoozed, and not moved again since; Undo of a set takes it away, of a move puts its time and words back, of a cancel brings it back, while there's room for it among those still to come (a deleted note's, on its task). An Undo puts a reminder's time back as it was, even one gone by meanwhile: it then comes at once, marked late. A reminder the same answer cancels makes room for one it sets, and a note it deletes for one it adds. An Undo of a move looks for later moves after its own message (an act can read the same as an earlier one).
- Undo takes back a tick (one of yours it finished opens again; only its own tick: the record its piece's keep made while that's the newest, or the step still ticked by hand, which a later tick by hand from Day looks the same as, a step keeping no time of its tick, `ChatActs.tickedBy`, 4.1.2), ticks an untick again, opens a finished task again or finishes a reopened one as it stood, restores a deleted task with the cards that added it, and puts a setting back.
- Once something else has changed it, its Undo says it can't be undone any more.

## Minutes he sets, and your pace

Minutes he sets (a new task's, a Minutes change, each step's) are planned exactly as he sets them: your pace stretches only the app's own estimates (`Calibration.multiplierFor`). So he allows for your pace himself.
- Where he sizes steps for a task the app estimated, he gives a Minutes change for the new total, marked `paced`: the app's estimate at your pace. Where you say how long something takes, he uses your minutes, unpaced.
- Only the chat's schema has `paced`. Reading his answer, the app keeps with a paced Minutes change the app's estimate it came from (`Change.pacedFrom`, the task's `appEffortMin`) and the task's content at that moment (`Change.pacedHash`, `Enrichments.inputHash`), in `InstructionAnswers.change`.
- On the task they're `mine.minutes.paced` (`from` and `contentHash`; `TaskItem.pacedMinutes`), planned as given like any minutes of yours.
- While the content is unchanged (`TaskItem.pacedTeaches`), finishing it is measured against that estimate, so your pace still learns from it; a re-read for newer prompts keeps the content the same.
- New content (an instruction, an email, your answers, a photo) may mean new work: it then teaches nothing, as a total of your own never does (`CompletionRecord.yourEstimate`).
- He's told when the app now estimates otherwise, or the task has changed: "as given: the app's 60 at their pace, which it now puts at 75". The task's sheet says "The app's 1 h at your pace", and the list's pill "Minutes at your pace".

## Web research
