# Felix's speech: the ideas, evaluated

Every idea considered on 28 Oct 2026, with what it rests on ([research.md](research.md), [evidence.md](evidence.md)), what it costs in calls and code, what could go wrong, and a verdict: **do**, **test** (an experiment in the harness before deciding), **owner** (a change of taste or policy for the owner to call), or **no**. The ranking is in [README.md](README.md) §What to change.

Cost figures: a Felix message costs about a cent with the cache (69 answers for $0.73, run 2); a chat answer $0.07 to $0.16; Haiku 5.5 is a fortieth of Opus 5.5's price.

## A. The prompt, what he's told, and the mechanics (I-01 to I-20)

**I-01. Brief him as a person, not as the app's writer.** *Do.* The system prompt opens "You write the short messages the planner app sends…" and goes on to describe inputs and a schema; the user turn is a labelled form. Rewrite the opening as who he is and his situation (22 and about their age, their closest mate and the one in charge of the two, their planner open on his phone, texting them about it), and keep the app's facts as "the details" after the story (I-15). Evidence: Anthropic's role guidance; prompt style bleeds into output style. Code: `FelixPrompts.system` and `describe`; `ChatPrompts.system`'s first paragraph. No cost. Risk: none the harness won't show.

**I-02. The voice shown by contrast pairs with reasons, not by ban lists.** *Do; the example mode (none, fixed, rotated) tested.* Replace the forty prohibitions with a positive description (a page at most) and five or six pairs: the app's line beside his, one sentence on why his is his (one thought; one number at most; says what a person would say about it, not what the app knows). Marked "the difference, not the words". Evidence: positive examples beat "don't" instructions (model guide); one example with its rationale is the documented fix for reproduced text (Fable 5.1 guide); examples copied in runs 2 and 3, skeletons copied since. Rotation: a pool of twelve pairs, three drawn at random a call, placed in the user turn so the cache holds. Risk: the pairs' own skeletons copied; rotation and the measures (I-17) catch it.

**I-03. His own notes fed back.** *Do.* `ChatMessage.feeling` is kept on each message of his and "never sent back to him". Show him today's notes beside his recent messages ("17:40, you felt: irritated, two gone…"), so an evening has a thread of mood, and the recovery message knows what it's recovering from. Code: `describe`'s recent block; the field is already there. No cost. Risk: he quotes his note; the prompt says the notes are his and not to say back, as the reasons were, and he obeyed that.

**I-04. The register written in his register.** *Do.* `REGISTER` is a rule-book paragraph with thirty quoted phrases; the prompt's style shapes the answer's. Write the voice section the way he'd text it (lower case, short lines) and keep the app's rules (the schema, the reasons, the rebalance) in the app's prose, separated. Evidence: "match your prompt style to the output style you want". No cost. Risk: a prompt in his voice tempts him to copy it; the pairs are marked and the measures watch.

**I-05. The voice reminder in the user turn.** *Test.* The Fable 5.1 guide: style instructions in the first user turn hold better than in the system prompt. Unknown for Opus 5.5. Put the two-line reminder of the moment's voice ("one thought; you're not impressed; no full stop") at the end of the story. Cost: a few cached-prefix tokens lost, none in money. Risk: none.

**I-06. The full stop as his cold register.** *Do.* Drop "a full stop between thoughts". Tell him a text ends with no full stop, and that the full stop is what he does when he's cold with them (firm and strong). Evidence: a one-word text ending in a full stop rated less sincere (Gunraj et al. 2016, small); the owner's own texts end in one a third of the time; 51 of 59 of his do. No cost. Risk: `FelixAnswers.message` cuts a long message at a sentence end; a message with no final full stop is cut at a word with "…" when over the limit, so the cut rule needs to accept a last line without one. Small.

**I-07. One thought a text, up to two texts a look.** *Do.* The schema's one `message` becomes the chosen one of three texts (I-10), `texts[send - 1]`, split at its newline into up to two bubbles: no new field, nothing nullable. The outbox sends both as chat messages and one notification (it already lays several lines newest first in 360 characters). Evidence: texts average about 35 characters (old, US); the owner's median 14 words; delays and bubbles raise perceived humanness (bubbles not isolated by any study). Code: `FelixAnswers`, `Outbox.send` twice with the same `kind` and `pieceKey`, `FelixRules.sent` counting the look once, the chat's history treating the pair as one nudge. Risk: his rules count messages (nudges, `said`); the pair must count as one. Medium code, no cost.

**I-08. The owner's thumbs into the prompt, digested.** *Do, after a fortnight of ratings.* Raw feedback stays unseen (honest marking). A weekly call, shaped like `MemoryTidy`, reads the fortnight's rated messages (`felix_feedback.py` already gathers them) and writes at most five lines for the profile block under "What they've told you about how you text" ("the clock time in every kept message grates"; "the firm lines they liked said what they'd promised"), and picks the marked-up messages into the pair pool (I-02) with the owner's note as the reason. Evidence: TICL, explanations carry up to 77% of personalisation's gain; HyPerAlign. Cost: one call a week, Opus at a few cents, or Haiku. Code: a `FeedbackDigest` beside `MemoryTidy`, its golden, `Profile`'s block; `ArchitectureTest` names its writer. Risk: the digest overfits to one bad night; it's a ceiling of five lines, replaced weekly, and the owner reads it in Settings. D-79: the pool lives on the phone and in `private/`, never in the repository.

**I-09. A register that mirrors the owner.** *Test, blind.* The persona is lower case and textisms; the owner writes capitalised full sentences with few. Style matching predicts relationships continuing (people, not bots). Variant: "write a bit like they do: capitals, whole sentences, the odd 'prob'", with their own last three messages shown as the sample. Risk: the owner chose the current voice and liked its firm lines; this is their taste to judge, so it's a blind pair in the owner's read, not a change.

**I-10. Three texts in one answer, he sends one.** *Do.* Schema: `texts` (three strings, different in what they pick out and in shape) and `send` (which). Evidence: verbalized sampling, 1.6–2.1× diversity with quality held, better on capable models; the one-field structured answer is the format that collapses diversity most; no temperature exists on Opus 5.5. Cost: output tokens roughly threefold on the message part, a fraction of a cent. Code: `FelixPrompts.SCHEMA`, `FelixAnswers.parse`, `FelixCaller.Result` keeping the unsent two for I-19; `SchemaCompileTest` live. Risk: the three are the same line thrice; the prompt asks for three that differ in what they pick out, and the measures (I-17) report overlap between them.

**I-11. A cheap second pass that rewrites.** *No, for now.* Haiku 5.5 reads the chosen text and, where it reads as an app, asks Opus to rewrite once. Judges drift to vibes and prefer their own family; a rewrite loop doubles latency on a message someone's waiting for (SlotStart is written ahead, the rest at the moment). Use the judge in the harness (I-17) and let the owner's thumbs do the judging in use (I-08).

**I-12. Haiku 5.5 as the writer.** *No.* A fortieth of the cost, but Felix's calls are already about a cent, and the literal, cheaper models over-apply style directives; the voice is the one thing worth the Opus price here.

**I-13. Effort and model experiments.** *Test.* Felix runs at medium effort with adaptive thinking (`ModelEffort.of(Job.Felix)`); nothing else was ever tried on the voice. Run the final prompt at low, medium and high (`ai-effort` from the PC, or an `EVAL_EFFORT` knob), and Fable 5.1 at low effort beside Opus 5.5 at medium (run 9 tried Fable at one setting: sharper firm, flatter warm, 2.5× the cost; the guide says Fable at low is often competitive on cost per task). Cost: five runs of 27 cases, about $5. Decide by the measures, the judge and the owner's blind read.

**I-14. A diary he reads.** *Do.* A line a day for the last seven, written by the app from `ActivityLog` (120 days kept) with no call: "tue: 3 of 4 done, the essay slipped to wed", "wed: all 4, essay hour in", "thu: nothing (ill, their memory says)"; the weekly check-in's answers in their words (feel, avoided, in the way, energy: stored, never sent); the week's memories with their dates. In the user turn, after the story. Evidence: memory raised engagement and perceived intelligence in companion studies; recall in his own words, not quotes; facts before feelings; MemoryBank's daily summaries. The level rule still resets at midnight, so he knows the week and is neutral about it: the prompt says so. Risk: shame on a bad week; see README §Risks. Code: `Looks` builds it, `describe` takes it; `ChatPrompts.plan` gets the same lines. No cost beyond tokens.

**I-15. The story of tonight.** *Do.* Replace the form's counts with an account in order: his nudges and their replies (he has these), the chat's last exchange (I-16), what got done and when, what slipped and what they'd said they'd do about it (a reply like "starting now" or "at 7" is a promise; he already sees it), the timer, a reason given. Then the ceiling in a person's words ("at most: not impressed"), then the details block as it is for the facts he may need (the piece, its text, the due date, what's left). Code: `describe` reordered and a `story` builder in `Looks` from the chat's messages, the records and the timer; the chat's `plan` the same. The counts stay in the details. No cost. Risk: `FelixTest` reads `describe` by line index; its asserts move.

**I-16. The chat's last exchanges in the notification prompt.** *Do.* `ChatHistory.his` filters his chat answers out, so a notification doesn't know what they agreed an hour ago. Add the last two exchanges (their message, his answer) to the story. Code: `describe`'s recent block takes `kind == Chat` pairs too. No cost. Risk: none; the chat already sends his nudges the other way.

**I-17. The harness's judge and measures.** *Do.* Replace the hand-kept lists as the deciders (keep them as regression flags): `run_stats.py`'s measures in `EvalRunTest`'s report (numbers a message, clock times, final full stops, shared 3- and 4-grams across cases, word overlap between attempts of a case, the commonest endings), and a Haiku 5.5 judge asked one anchored question per message, blind to the prompt. Evidence: stylometry (uniformity is the tell), judge research (short rubric, decomposed, deterministic checks keep authority). Cost: Haiku at a few thousandths of a cent a message (about 200 tokens in, 15 out). Code: `EvalRunTest`'s mood set.

**I-18. Numbers only where they're the point.** *Do (overturns D-57's "exact where it matters").* "in at 9 tomorrow" when the deadline is the thing; else "tonight", "the physics", "later"; never minutes, a time and a date in one text; the Day's cards carry the exact times (they do). Risk: the owner valued exactness (D-57); a time they gave ("at 7") is still said, as a promise is. The thumbs will say.

**I-19. The unsent candidates in the feedback dialog.** *Do with I-10.* The rating dialog shows his note already; show the two texts he didn't send and let the owner tap the one they'd rather have had. A pairwise preference beats a thumb for learning taste (it's what preference data is). Stored on the message (`preferred`), read by `felix_feedback.py`, fed to I-08. Code: `ChatMessage` gains `unsent` (plain strings) and `preferred`; `chat.json` 11, nothing migrated; `AskActivity`'s dialog. Risk: the schema and store grow; small.

**I-20. The morning as the app's list and his one line.** *Owner.* The morning is "the whole briefing, with no list of the day beside it" (D-57), a 480-character list in his voice, the most app-shaped thing he writes. The app already has the list in its own words for when Claude's off (`FelixWords.morningList`). Show that as the card and have him add one line a person would ("two things tonight, both short. essay's the one"). Cost: the morning call gets cheaper. Risk: the owner chose the briefing as his; their call.

## B. Behaviour (I-21 to I-28)

**I-21. Teasing and a joke beside the answer, when warm.** *Owner.* "No sarcasm at them" and "never a joke in place of the answer" leave him no humour. Allow light teasing at neutral and pleased, never when let down, never about who they are; a joke beside the answer, not instead of it. Evidence: friends converge on affiliative humour (adolescents); no study on banter and intimacy among young men was found. Risk: a joke on a bad night reads as sarcasm; the level gates it. The owner's taste.

**I-22. A non-work line now and then.** *Owner.* A friend isn't only about the work: "how was the match" when the calendar had one, "cold any better" when a memory says so. Once a day at most, in the morning or a day-done, never in a nudge. No call: it rides on an existing message. Evidence: familiarity enjoyed once rapport is built through low-stakes facts (Cox et al. 2025); intrusive when it opens with feelings. Risk: cringe; the owner's read.

**I-23. Replying in kind.** *Owner.* A reply of theirs that needs nothing ("ok starting", "done") gets nothing back today, or a chat call. A friend sends "nice" or "go on then". A one-liner is a Chat call (a few cents) or the app's word (a fixed line, which reads as a bot by the third time). Owner's call on the cost; not the app's words.

**I-24. Silence as a message.** *Already there.* He may give an empty message; the rules count it as said. Keep. A friend doesn't text at every slot.

**I-25. A life of his own.** *No.* Self-disclosure ("i'm shattered too") is what mates do, and research on reciprocity in bots is thin; a fabricated life is the AI tell the owner wants gone. Nothing invented about him.

**I-26. Typos and self-corrections.** *No.* Typos lowered perceived humanness in a chatbot (Westerman et al. 2018). Typing delays did raise it, but a notification can't be delayed and the chat's three dots already are one.

**I-27. Emojis.** *Owner, no change proposed.* Banned by the owner's taste. People his age use a few; its absence is a mild tell. Left as is.

**I-28. The nickname and address words.** *Keep.* The owner said their rate isn't a problem; via a memory only (Q4).

## C. Structure and calls (I-29, I-30)

**I-29. Shorter system prompts.** *Do, with I-01 and I-02.* The notification prompt is 1,914 words for a 240-character answer; the chat's 3,855, the voice a fifth of it and last. Halve the notification prompt (the capabilities list stays in the chat's). The cached prefix shrinks; literal over-application has less to over-apply.

**I-30. The feeling line in the system block, the ceiling in the user turn, both in a person's words.** *Do with I-15.* `Mood.line` says "How you feel, at most: let down, firmly (2 slots missed today; …)". Keep the level, say it as a person would ("at most: not impressed", "at most: properly fed up", "pleased", "really pleased") and drop the bracketed reasons, which the story now carries. `FelixTest` asserts the line's text; it changes.
