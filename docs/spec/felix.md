# Felix

Felix is the app's voice: the persona Claude writes as, in the chat and in the messages the app sends about your work. A strict friend who knows your week; Claude is the model behind him.
He can change almost anything about your work, each change made as he gives it with an Undo, and he never touches the blocker, protection or Claude's own settings.
Everything each of his calls is sent, circumstance by circumstance and with examples, is in [felix-inputs.md](felix-inputs.md).
His code is in `felix/`: `Conversation` (the chat, a reply to his notification, what he remembers, the week's check-in, what the app says in his name), `Looks` (what he writes unprompted) and `Outbox` (every message in his name into the chat first, and his one notification made from it), each built like `Focus`: its stores, the clock, and `FelixPorts` for what Android does. `ConversationTest`, `LooksTest` and `OutboxTest` drive them on temporary stores, his calls answered by a stand-in for Claude's API (`FelixHarness`).
The chat is his one visible voice (D-95): every word in his name is a message there, and a notification of his is made from it, never beside it.

## Who he is

- His name and the line that says who he is are settings (`felixName`, `felixLine`, by default "your best mate, the one who keeps you on it", from 4.7.0; a phone keeps the line it has stored), changed in Settings → Felix or by him; every prompt of his names him with them (AI-22).
- Who he is and how he writes (D-57, D-119, D-120): both his prompts open with who he is to you (`FelixPrompts.who`): 22, British, middle-class, about your age and the closest person you've got, in the shape of a stern boyfriend's with the romance taken out (he cares how you're doing rather than how the plan's doing, he's cheeky with you, and he's the one in charge because you want it that way), nothing sexual between you, ever; his line from Settings quoted as how the app describes him to you. No year of yours is said to him: his calls' profile has the year out of its first line ("A UK sixth-form student."), all else as the readers' (`Profile.Block.felix`). How he texts is written in his own register (`FelixPrompts.REGISTER`, in both prompts): one thought a text, a second thing a second text, never a paragraph; lower case, contractions, the odd "tbh" or "ngl", never London's words ("proper", "sorted", "nah", "alr"), no emojis; now and then "mate", "bro", "bud" or "dude", or what his memories say to call you, never when he's properly fed up; no full stop at a text's end, the full stop being what he does when he's cold; a number only where it's the point (a time you gave, the day something's in), never minutes, a time and a date in one text, never a time in words; nothing in the app's words, no customer service, no catchphrases, none of the lines you've said grate ("who are you (and what have you done with my mate)", "knew you had it in you", "off your plate", "done and dusted", "two days to spare"); a dig or a wind-up beside the point, and when he's properly fed up only a mock threat nobody would take as real, never physical (Q112); praise about the particular thing, a plain word of it after ("…, nice"); no praise for nothing, no promise the app can't keep. The difference is shown by pairs, the app's line beside his with why his is his, never words to reuse (`FelixPrompts.PAIRS`, the chat's `ChatPrompts.PAIRS`), not by lists of what not to say. His own messages open with a quick greeting now and then, on the app's word: where nothing's been said in the chat for three hours (`FelixPrompts.GREET_AFTER_MIN`), as each is written on its own (4.3.11); and his nudges say which piece it is the way you'd say it, and what's worth knowing where that's the point, never how to do it unless you ask, a reminder you set still said as you asked it: he's told its first action, so he knows what it is, but doesn't pass it on (4.3.9). A message that's just talk gets a reply and nothing else (`FelixPrompts.JUST_TALK`). The app's own lines in his name (`FelixWords`) are in the same register, and his chips too, the app taking either their words or its own.
- How he feels (D-119, `felix/Mood.kt`, pure, `MoodTest`): a level the app works out for each message from today's records, his nudges and the task (`Mood.of`). Let down firmly at two slots missed or a piece's second nudge (a silent one isn't counted), strongly at three; a step more where a real deadline is within 24 hours with nothing done on it (no minutes worked, no photo's, no step ticked), only on a day with a miss or a piece already nudged; pleased at a slot kept; proud at the day done with every slot kept (two or more, none made up late), or at a missed piece made up (a `KeptLate` record after its slot's end, with his SlotEnd about it) with no other miss. A SlotStart or SlotEnd takes the let-down side, a SlotKept or DayDone the other, the chat's turn the side today's newest record is on; the morning and what he only passes on (a reminder, a question, completions, the check-in, the summary) are neutral, so each day starts clean. He's told it in one line in both prompts, under today's story, in a person's words ("How you feel, at most: properly fed up"; not impressed, pleased, really pleased: `Mood.line`, D-120), none at neutral, and nothing of what it rests on, which the story carries: a ceiling, which a reason given, an illness or a hard week he remembers, a stressed or upset reply or a standing instruction brings down, and nothing takes higher (`FelixPrompts.FEELING`, after the register in both prompts; the standing instructions come after both, so they win). The feeling is as much a message's job as what it says, carried by the whole of it (what he picks out, how long, how warm or flat, whether he asks or tells) rather than named; let down, he says the pointed thing (what you'd said you'd do, how small it was, that it's another) and what he wants, and once the work starts it's over. He writes a few words to himself first, on how he feels and what he wants you to feel (`feeling`, first in each answer of his, the chat's too: `FelixPrompts.NOTE`), and the message from them; one that only passes something on stays plain. The level a message of his was written at is kept on it (`ChatMessage.mood`), so a slot kept after a firm or strong message about its task today is always answered, above `FelixRules.due`'s early exits (`Mood.letDown`); his note is kept too (`ChatMessage.feeling`, on `FelixAnswer` for one written ahead), shown in the chat only as you rate the message (below), and told him again beside his message in his story of that day (D-120), so an evening has a thread of how he's felt. Your rating, your words and your pick are never sent to him, nor turned into anything he's sent: only an agent reading them off the phone acts on them (D-120).
- Settings → Felix also has how far into a slot he asks (`felixGraceMin`, 10 minutes), when he checks again if he doesn't say (`felixDefaultCheckMin`, 20), and whether he may search the web (`felixSearches`, on).
- A message of his is `Speaker.Felix` (from 4.0.1), stored as "Claude", as it was before; the screens show his name.
- His button is on every screen: Day, Work, Stats, Settings, a task's sheet (*Ask Felix about it*), and the block screen, about the next piece's task (UI-16). It wears the count of his messages unread (`Conversation.unread`): those since you last left the chat, but a question you've answered, or a message you've replied to, from his notification (`Outbox.repliedTo`; only that one: what the notification left out stays unread, 4.1.2).
- While Claude is off (`ModelGateway.claudeKey` null) he writes nothing unprompted of his own, and the chat says why your message waits; the app's own words still go in his name: the morning's list, how work went, the check-in's and a calendar event's questions, and your reminders, in your words.

## The chat

`ui/AskActivity.kt` has two tabs, *Chat* and *Your changes*; its menu has *Mark all answered*, *What Felix remembers* and *Clear the chat*. The chat is on screen only on its tab: *Your changes* shown, it's left as on leaving the screen, and back, opened again; every way into the screen, his notification's tap among them, shows the *Chat* tab. The chat holds every message of his, what he writes unasked among them, and every one is kept (D-96):
- the live window in `chat.json`: the newest 200 messages or those of the last 14 days, whichever keeps more (D-96), and every one of his still unread; what's past it and read moves to the archive, a file a month (`chat-archive-<yyyy-MM>.json`, `ChatArchiveStore`), oldest first, the archive written before the window is trimmed (`Outbox.moveWindow`), and only as it was copied: one changed meanwhile (a question answered as the archive was written) stays to move as it then is (4.3.16);
- clearing the chat empties both, and leaves what it made and his state;
- it reads as a messaging app's (`ui/ChatScreenState.kt`): each day's date over its first message ("Today", "Yesterday", "Tuesday 13 October"), the time under each, and a *New messages* line over the first of his messages unread when it was opened, for that visit, one you've replied to from his notification not among them, and his notification's tap opening it at the first you haven't (4.1.2);
- it opens at that message, or, from his notification, at the message the notification was for, tinted for a moment, else at its foot; leaving it, what's in it is read (`ChatState.readAt`, `Conversation.read`);
- scrolled back to its top, the archive's messages show, a page of 100 at a time (*Earlier messages*), never put back in the window: its newest months read as far as the pages go, off the main thread, and none at all till you scroll back (whether it holds any is said by whether it has a month's file); a reply on screen quoting one of its messages has that message read for its quote, and a quote tapped further back reads the months back to it; They're shown with nothing to do on them (their cards' and lines' Undo are the window's), and a reply to one is routed and quoted as any is (`Outbox.find`), a question answered from there marked answered there.
- a message, his or yours, swiped right past a third of the width (with a haptic tick) or held for *Reply* is the one your next replies to: a strip over the box says whose and its first line, with ✕ to reply to none. Sent, yours carries it (`replyTo`) and shows it over your words, tinted, where it isn't the message just above; tapped, the chat goes to it, from the archive too. Held, *Copy* copies a message's words.
- a message of his held takes *Thumbs up* or *Thumbs down* (D-119): a box shows his words and, where one was kept, his note on how he felt as he wrote them, and the texts he wrote beside them and didn't send (D-120), one of which you may tap as the one you'd rather have had (tapped again, none), kept in view as you write what was good or what's off, which may be nothing; saved, a small thumb shows by its time, and a tap on it opens the box again, to change it or *Remove* it. `Conversation.rate` keeps it on the message (`rating`, "Up" or "Down", `feedback`, `ratedAt`, and your pick, `preferred`, one of its `unsent` or none), in the live chat or the archive's month that holds it (`Outbox.changeInArchive`, `ChatArchiveStore.change`); one of yours isn't rated, and nothing follows: it isn't a turn of the chat, wakes no notification, and is never sent to him. It's read off the phone, with his note and the level each message was written at, by `private/tools/felix_feedback.py`, to tune his prompts; clearing the chat lets it go with the messages.
- when a question of his still waits for its answer since your last turn of the chat (the check-in's under way, the one it asked last, never one from a week gone; a reading's, how work went, a calendar event's: `Replies.pending`), the box and his chips answer it, its strip over the box by itself, and its ✕ sends your words to him instead (D-98); with several asked together (how work went, a calendar's questions), the one before is next once one's answered. His newest message still waiting since your last turn (below), a nudge as much as a question, takes the box and the chips: a nudge just above the box with no strip, your words its reply (`replyTo`) taking none of its subject, so they're told him as they are (`Conversation.ask`'s `takeItsSubject`), one further up with its strip; an older question waits, shown so, and is the box's again once the nudge is marked answered (4.0.10).
- his notification holds his unread messages, as many as fit about 360 characters, the newest first, each ended at a whole sentence where it runs past that (`Outbox.NOTICE_ROOM`): One UI holds an expanded notification's height and cuts off what runs past it, the Reply and chips with it; the older ones are in the chat, which a tap opens at the first unread you haven't replied to;
- his messages waiting for your answer say so under them, "waiting for your answer" by the time (D-99, `Replies.awaitingIds`): his questions above till they're answered or a message of yours replies to them (a calendar event's only till the event begins), and his nudges with replies of their own, each till you reply to it, a newer message of his is about the same task (or, about none, of the same kind), or its day is over; tapped, that line is the message's *Reply*. A reply changes nothing of his in the store: a message of his waits no more once one of yours replies to it (`ChatMessage.replyTo`), so his notification still shows your words under it while he answers. Held, *Mark answered* lets one go, and the menu's *Mark all answered* every one, with an Undo for six seconds (`Conversation.markAnswered`: answered, so out of his notification too); a check-in's question while the check-in's under way isn't let go. He marks his nudges too (4.2.2): the chat's context lists those still waiting by id (the newest ten), and where your words answer one without replying to it, his answer names it (`answered`), marked as *Mark answered* marks it, at his word, and said under his answer ("Taken as answered: …", with no Undo); an id he names that wasn't listed is dropped. A question whose answer the app keeps (a reading's, how work went, a calendar event's, the check-in's) and a reminder aren't listed: each waits for its reply, which records its answer.
- under an answer of his, what he did is folded (`ChatSummary`): his words alone, and a chevron at the bubble's corner, red where something went wrong (not made, not looked up, not brought, left out). Open (the chevron, or a tap on his bubble), his cards, acts, what he remembered, his searches and what was left out show as they always did, with every Undo; leaving the chat closes every one. A task he shows you, his chips and a button in the app's words stay outside the fold. A message of his that arrives while the chat is on screen, or was unread when it opened, has a label slide out from behind its bubble for five seconds ("Changes: 2 · Remembered · Searched", what went wrong in red), once; tapped, it opens the fold. A message that's just talk has neither.
- You write by keyboard or voice (UI-07). A message from a task's, an event's or a day's page carries what it's about (`About`).
- A message sent while he answers another of yours lets that answer go, paid for and unused, with nothing more looked up or read for it, and he answers both together (D-98); what waits, waits together, the newest saying why (taken back, the one before it says it), and what you sent waiting is sent to him with it, a reply to an older nudge too; one past the conversation's last 12 says it was left out, and waits for its *Try again*. While Claude can't be asked (off, resting, at its cap) yours says why, and it's answered once Claude can be: the chat opened again, *Try again*, Claude back, or after a sync.
- While he answers, three dots show; after 10 seconds *Cancel* lets the answer go (AC-08). A call once sent is paid for, so it isn't closed.
- An answer the app can't use (declined, cut off, unreadable) waits for *Try again*, as each try is paid for.
- His suggested replies, up to three (`suggestedReplies`), are chips under his last message, the message the box answers, or one of his you chose to reply to (above) (UI-06); a tap is a reply to it, as one from anywhere is (below): a check-in's, a calendar question's or how work went with no call.
- A message in the app's words can carry a button of its own (`ChatMessage.opens`): the week's review to open, its summary to send.

`felix/ChatReader.kt` makes the chat's call (`ChatReader.answer`): one for a message, and one for each round of what he asks for (two at most, `ChatRounds.MAX_ROUNDS`), at medium effort (AC-02), under the cap (AC-05). Someone is waiting, so it goes through a rest after a failure, but not past a key problem or the cap (AC-04).
- `ChatPrompts.system`: who he is (`FelixPrompts.who`, as his messages have it), what he can do (below), and how to answer: one to three short sentences saying what he's done, one short question and no change when he needs to know more, and nothing he isn't asked for.
- `ChatPrompts.turns`: what `ChatPrompts.history` takes of the chat (D-97): the conversation's last 12 messages (`HISTORY`: yours, and his answers to you) and his last two nudges (`NUDGES`: what he wrote unasked), each with your reply, in their order, starting with one of yours; each earlier answer with its cards and what became of them; a nudge before any of yours said in your first turn. A reply that went to no call (a check-in's answer) goes only with its nudge, so nudges can't push the conversation out. The message being answered is the newest of yours: what he wrote unasked after it isn't sent with it. A message of yours that replies to one that isn't just before it starts with that one, quoted from the whole chat ("(Replying to your message: “…”)"), and the prompt says that's what it answers; a message the app wrote in his name is marked "(the app wrote this for you)".
- All of it within 40 000 characters (`Pricing.MAX_INPUT_CHARS`): your newest message whole, earlier turns in up to a quarter of the rest, then the context, then the plan, which keeps at least a third of their room.
- `ChatAnswers.parse` checks his answer against what there is. What can't be used is left out and said under the reply ("Left out, as the app can't use it: …").

## What he's told

With your newest message, from one snapshot of the tasks, the calendar and your changes, which his answer is then checked against (`Conversation.chatContext`):
- `ChatPrompts.context`: today and the time; the open tasks (id, title, source, its class and the subject the class map gives it (4.1.1), kind, due date and whether it's a real deadline or a plan, minutes, minutes worked, can't start until), each with its steps by place;
- tasks that look like the same work (DX-15), the fortnight's calendar events, the changes in use, your own tasks finished in the last week, and the settings he may change, with their values;
- **each class's next lessons** (G8, 4.1.1, `core/Lessons.kt`): its next three, after the calendar's events, from Power Planner's timetable as last read (DX-09), looked for as far as the plan's reach so a holiday doesn't hide them, none on a day of no school nor from a half day's end; a Power Planner class and a Teams code with the same subject in Settings → About you's classes are one class (that list gains the timetable's classes), as is a Teams code given a Power Planner class's own name (the class it is, nothing mapped back), one with no subject tied to no code; dated where the timetable's over a week old, or said to be of no known age where its last read's time is lost, left out with none read;
- **your pace**: the multipliers the calibration has learned, by kind and class, with how many timed tasks each rests on, or that nothing's learned yet;
- what each task's minutes come to in the plan: "planned as 54 at their pace" for the app's estimates, "as given" for your minutes and for steps you or he gave, and a paced total as below;
- **each open task's own text** (D-93, `enrich/TaskText.kt`): an assignment's instructions, an email's conversation, a planner item's details, your notes, each at most 600 characters (`ChatPrompts.BRIEF_TEXT`, 12 000 in all), what's attached, its links, an email's sender and the whole text's length on its line in the list (`TaskText.holds`, 160 characters a line, 6 000 in all, past that its length alone); last in the context, so it's what a long one loses first. A longer text is its reading's summary of all of it (`Enrichment.summary`, [readings.md](readings.md)); past the 16 000 characters a reading sees (`ReadingPrompts.MAX_TEXT`), of its start, with its end after it; with none (Claude off when it was read, or read before summaries), its start and its end, saying how much of the middle was left out: a conversation is kept oldest first, so its end holds the newest replies;
- `ChatPrompts.plan`: the plan as Day shows it: the slot under way or the next thing to do, with a timer running on its task (", timer on it since 17:02, 8 min left", D-112), or a timer on another's in a line of its own (his own messages have the same, `FelixPrompts.describe`); whether blocking applies and why, by the plan he's shown (a slot missed today with no time left today keeping it on till the day's hours end, BL-11), the free time and what finishing earns (G5, 4.1.1, `Focus.blockingLine`); today's slots each past, now or ahead (AI-13), with their keys, and today's breaks outside among them, under way or to come, each by its own time (D-116); the next piece of each task with no steps, by key, and the pieces kept today (G1); what isn't planned and why, other events, and what's planned past the fortnight; under today's slots, today's story beside the chat (today's slots as they went and his messages written unasked, with his notes and your replies; the chat's own exchanges are its turns), how he feels, at most, by the side today's newest record is on (D-119, above), and his diary (D-120, below);
- then each of the plan's next 14 days, with its minutes against its working time and each piece's reason, the week's with their breaks outside;
- the profile every call carries (AI-05, AI-06; [privacy.md](privacy.md)).

What your message is about comes with it in full, before your words (D-93): a task's whole text (`ChatPrompts.WHOLE_TEXT`, 8 000 characters; past that, its reading's summary, then its start and end) with your answers to its questions, or an event's place and description.

**Asking for more** (`details`): beside tasks' texts, the week's numbers (`stats`, G6: this week so far and last week, by subject, `Stats.weekLines`) and the calendar past the fortnight (`calendar`, the four weeks after it, or `calendar:YYYY-MM`, a month, up to 60 events, saying where the calendar's read ends: `ChatPrompts.calendarBlock`, G7), each once, said under his answer ("Read: the week's numbers."); his prompt's item 7 says what he can ask for and when (D-102). Two spans of the calendar a message at most (`ChatRounds.MAX_SPANS`); what's brought beside the texts has a room of its own, 12 000 characters, a block past its share cut at a whole line, saying how many lines were left out. The last round a message has is told it's the last; what he still asks for after it is said under his answer ("Not brought: …", in red), and a search that couldn't be made is told to the next round, so he doesn't ask for it again.

**Reading a task in full** (`details`, D-93): where he needs more of a task's text than the context gives, his answer names it (three at most, never the one the message is about, which came whole) and says only that he's reading it; he's asked again with their whole texts (each an even share of 16 000 characters, 8 000 at most) after your words, and that answer is his, with "Read in full: …" under it. Asked for a web search too, he's asked once with both; where the texts show a need for a search, or the search for a text, once more, never more (`ChatRounds`). The texts he read are kept till he's answered with them, so a failed answer is asked again with them, not for them again.

## What he can do

Everything he gives is made as his answer comes, each with its Undo (next section).
- **New tasks of your own** (`newTasks`, at most ten): title, kind (Homework, Revision, Admin or Event), subject, due date and time (with no time, an event's is 09:00, anything else's the end of the day), start, minutes, notes, and steps for a big one, each saying whether it needs the steps before it done first (`anyOrder`, D-109). Each is checked as the form checks one (`OwnTaskRules.problem`), after the chat's own check that every date in it, its steps' too, is within a month back and 400 days ahead (`ChatAnswers`).
- **Changes** (`changes`, at most 40), of an instruction's types (`InstructionPrompts.CHANGE_TYPES`): a task isn't one, can't start until a date, waits for another, is due by a date, takes N minutes, goes on one day, has its next piece at a time, is another's work, has these steps or this name;
- and for days and events: how long an event takes, a day's limit, busy times (each named from your words where they give it, "Football", and written to your calendar as "Busy: Football" to block the time out, D-117), days off and half-days (into the school calendar, PL-16);
- a day kept for only some tasks (`DayOnly`, D-115): one change a task, each with its date; told nothing of what else may come in, he makes it letting in nothing and asks in the same reply, offering "Nothing else", "Anki too" and "Anki and deadlines" as quick replies, the answer one more DayOnly for that day with its `alsoAllow`. The instruction reader makes it too, letting in nothing where your words don't say. His context shows each such day on its line of the plan (`ChatPrompts.onlyLine`: its tasks, those finished marked done, and what it lets in), and a task he puts on one is said to join it under his answer (`ChatAnswers.joined`).
- a day's breaks outside (D-116): one moved to a time that day (`OutsideAt`, naming it by its own time, "13:00", even once it's been moved; one added too, and one taken off, which is put back so), one or all of a day's taken off (`NoOutside`), or one added at a time on any day (`OutsideAdded`); each on a date, never a weekday, a break staying on its day. One the day hasn't, one begun, a time gone, one over another of the day's breaks or running past midnight, or one added at a time the day's own are named by is left out and said; his changes are checked in turn, so he can take one break off and move another into its time. The instruction reader makes them too, told the week's breaks by their own times (`InstructionPrompts.describe`). His context lists today's among today's slots ("Outside, the 13:00 break, moved by you") and the week's later days' on their lines, each by its own time and who moved or added it (`InstructionRules.outsideBy`); his own messages are told today's under way or to come (`FelixPrompts.describe`).
- **Steps** for any task but Anki's, whose steps are its cards (AI-19): laid over the source's and the reading's (`TaskItem.mine.steps`, set by him), ticks kept by id, each saying whether it needs the steps before it done first (`anyOrder`, D-109). They must add up to the task's minutes, or come with a Minutes change for the new total, or they're left out.
- **Memories** (`memories`): facts and standing instructions (below).
- **Actions** (`actions`, AI-14): tick or untick a step (its slot kept where the plan has one, earning as a tick does); finish, reopen or delete one of your own tasks (or feedback done by its tick, NF-06); open a task on a card, or where it lives (`open` with value `source`, G3: Teams, Power Planner, Gmail or AnkiDroid, as its sheet's Open does, at once, said under his answer, while the chat's on screen (left as it opens, its card's there too): else, from his notification's reply, a retry in the background or with the chat left, on a card, its sheet's Open opening it there); set a setting.
- **A piece kept, or taken back** (G1, 4.1.1): `keepPiece` keeps a piece that has no step to tick (a task in boxes, a slot of today's) as Day's Done keeps it (`Focus.keepSlot`), by a key the plan he's shown lists, as of that plan (one kept from Day while he answered isn't kept again, a box's next having its key); `unkeepPiece` takes back one kept today (`Focus.unkeepSlot`), one done ahead of its day too, the record he was shown (one kept again while he answered is yours), by a key from his context's *Kept today*; one that finished a task of yours (or one done by its tick) opens it again with it, as the keep's Undo does (one finished another way since stays so, and nothing's taken back); one not taken back for that, or as it was kept again while he answered, is said under his answer. - **How finished work went, with Claude off** (LR-05, `learn/Assessment.kt`): a message for each completion ("done: … how'd it go?"), with *harder*, *about right* and *easier*, his chips' words, the app taking those or its own (*Harder*, *As expected*, *Easier*) whatever their case (`FelixWords.chip`). With Claude on, they wait for his evening question.

## Notifications and replies

He has one notification (`NoticeId.Felix`), made by his outbox from the chat (`Outbox.notify`, `felix/FelixNotifications.kt`), on the *Felix* channel, in Android's messaging style: his unread messages (after `ChatState.readAt`, with words), oldest first, and under them your replies to them; a reply box answering the newest, its choices his replies. It's made again, not added to, whenever the chat changes: a new message of his sounds, a rebuild doesn't. Tapped, it opens the chat at the first of its messages you haven't replied to.
- Nothing's posted while the chat is on screen (its Chat tab, not Your changes), and opening the chat takes it away. A reply from it doesn't (it isn't auto-cancelled), and its Reply stays for the next: your words show there as a speaker's named "You", as Android hides the Reply while the newest message is the user's own.
- A question of his answered, here or elsewhere, leaves it.
- In quiet hours the week's review, its summary and a calendar event's question are in the chat at once and left out of it till the morning's briefing (`NotifyRules.waitsForMorning`): once the morning's message is written, those before it are in it each time it's made again, even where quiet hours outlast the briefing (`Outbox.notice`).

He has no daily ceiling. The app's routine channels (a calendar move put back, the check-in's reminder, Claude's) go at most four a day and never in quiet hours, the rest held for the next briefing (LR-07, `notify/NotifyRules.kt`).
Every notification of the app's is named once, with request codes of its own for its tap and buttons (`notify/NoticeId.kt`), and posted through `notify/Notifier.kt`, which applies those rules and alone posts and cancels them (`notify/Channels.kt` makes the channels; WorkManager shows the work-under-way ones from each worker's `ForegroundInfo`). The notifications his messages took the place of (`NoticeId.RETIRED`) are taken away at start, where one's left.

A reply, from his notification (`felix/FelixReplyReceiver.kt`), one of his chips, or a swipe or *Reply* in the chat, comes in one way (D-98: `Conversation.ask` with `replyTo`): it's kept in the chat under the message it answers (`ChatMessage.replyTo`) and routed by what that message is (`felix/Replies.kt`):
- a check-in's question: its next asked; a reading's question: an instruction on its task, as above; how work went: the answer on each completion; a calendar event's question: its answer kept; a reminder's *Done* or snooze: on it (4.1.0). Each with no call, but a reading's question's, which Claude reads (above): from his notification as work of its own (`Conversation.replyFromNotification`, `FelixReplyWorker`, 4.3.16).
- anything else: a chat turn, about what the message it answers was; from his notification, your words under his there while he answers, and his answer his next message, made as work of its own (`Conversation.replyFromNotification`, `FelixReplyWorker`). One sent while he answers another of yours goes with it, as one in the chat does (above).

## What he remembers

`core/Memory.kt` keeps `memory.json`: facts about you, each under a short topic that a newer fact replaces, and standing instructions on how he behaves (AI-21).
- The chat and his messages give at most five a time; an empty text forgets one. Busy times, limits and days off are changes, not memories.
- **For a while** (4.2.0, D-103): a fact or a rule can hold till a date (`until`: a date, through that day, or a date and time: "a cold this week", "not tonight" till 07:00), and a fact go with a task (`taskId`: "for the trip, I'm sharing a room"). An until gone by is refused, saying so; one more than a year ahead is cut to a year; a task not listed is said, and the fact kept without it. What's run out is forgotten silently (`Memories.expired`): past its until; a fortnight after its task finished; its task gone, once its delete can't be undone (a source's, once that source has been read since too). The same instruction given for another while takes the new until, its Undo bringing back the old. It's dropped before every profile is made, so he's never told a stale line, and let go from `memory.json` there, before something's remembered, as Settings → Felix's list opens, before the tidy, on both sides of a restore before they're merged, and each morning before the briefing (`Conversation.forgetExpired`).
- Facts have 6 000 characters of room, the oldest going first; standing instructions at most 20, one more refused with a line saying so.
- Each is said under his answer ("Remembered: …", "Forgot: …", "Felix will: …", with how long it holds: ", until Sun 25 Oct", ", with “Trip consent form”"), with its *Undo* while what it did is unchanged (`Memories.undo`, the fact or rule compared whole, its until and task too), bringing back what it replaced or pushed out.
- Settings → Felix and the chat's menu list them all, each with how long it holds and its delete, and take a standing instruction in your own words (`ui/MemorySection.kt`).
- All of it goes in every call's profile: every standing instruction, and the facts newest first, a passing one saying so ("- cold: feeling rough (until Sun 25 Oct)").

**The tidy** (`enrich/MemoryTidy.kt`, its call `enrich/MemoryTidier.kt`): past 70 % of the room, Sunday's review sends the facts to Claude in a call of its own, which gives back at most two thirds as many lines.
They're kept only if they make room and the facts are unchanged meanwhile (`MemoryTidy.after`, `applied`). The facts ahead of the tidy and after it go with the week's review, whose page can undo it. Standing instructions are never tidied, nor a fact with an until or a task (`MemoryTidy.tidied`: they go by themselves, and a merge would lose when); they leave the rest less room, so it's due once the facts it may rewrite are past 70 % of the room the others leave (`MemoryTidy.due`), what's run out let go of first; an answer giving the topic of a fact it wasn't shown is refused (`MemoryTidy.withExpiring`).

## The instructions screen

*Your changes*, the chat's second tab, lists the changes in use, each with *Take back*, and those to check: still being read, or unclear with why (*Write again*, *Discard*).
It holds Felix's changes, the quick changes made on a task's sheet without Claude (`ui/TaskSheetState.kt`), and instructions read by `enrich/InstructionReader.kt`.
`tasks/Instructions.kt` keeps them in `instructions.json`: it has each read, applies or refuses it, takes it back, and lays what's in use over the tasks (`layTaskOverrides`), the calendar's answers and the plan (`layInstructions`). It's built like `Focus` (its stores, the clock, the gateway, the focus and the plan); `tasks/InstructionsTest` drives it on temporary stores, and `TaskSheetStateTest` the sheet's changes without Compose.
- `InstructionPrompts` reads your own words about a task, an event, a day or nothing in particular into changes of the chat's types, given the open tasks and the fortnight's events (`InstructionPrompts.describe`).
- It reads an instruction you write again, and your reply to Felix's question about a task, shown his question as he put it, else as its reading asked it, whatever has changed of the task since: it says whether the reply answers it (`answersQuestion`, 4.3.12), and gives an answer changes only for what it asks for (a day, a time, a total), none for what it only says of the work, which is kept with the task for its reading. It's told a reply that isn't an answer is answered in the chat, unless it asks for a change, made as any instruction's (4.3.19).
- `InstructionAnswers.parse` checks the reading: if one change can't be made, none are, and it says why.
- A reading is applied as soon as it's read, armed or not, and listed for you to check and take back. One that would make tasks wait for each other in a circle, put work after a deadline still to come (overdue work can go to any time, D-30), or leave one of your own as the form wouldn't take it is marked unclear instead.
- It's never told your pace, and its schema has no `paced`: its totals are always your own reckoning (`Change.pacedFrom` empty).
