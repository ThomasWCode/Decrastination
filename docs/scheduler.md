# Planner, block policy, anti-tamper, and learning

All four are pure Kotlin with an injected `Clock`, no Android types, unit-tested against `fixtures/`. Revised 7 Oct 2026 after your answers: strict blocking while anything is due today or tomorrow, earned free time otherwise, large tasks split into day-sized chunks, no bypass, and calibration from your own history.

## 1. Inputs

- `tasks`: open tasks with `availableFrom <= now`. Each has `dueAt?`, `effortMin`, `progress`, `kind`, `class`, and optionally an ordered list of **sub-steps** (from the LLM: "Q1–8 ~25 min", "Q9–16 ~25 min", "mark answers ~10 min") whose estimates sum to `effortMin`.
- `availability`: weekly template, your answer of 7 Oct: **Mon–Fri 16:45–22:00, Sat–Sun 08:30–22:30** (commute already inside the 16:45 start), minus calendar busy blocks (Phase 5), minus the part of today already gone.
- `calibration`: per-(kind, class) effort multipliers and safety margins learned from history (§5), defaults 1.0 and 1 day.
- `now`.

## 2. Effort

`remainingMin = effortMin × multiplier(kind, class) × (1 − progress)`, floored at 5. Effort comes from, in order: your override, the LLM estimate, a per-kind default (Homework 40, Admin 15, Event 0, Anki from card counts: reviews × 8 s, new × 25 s; since review v2's DX-11, the deck's own seconds a card where it's been learned from time in AnkiDroid, docs/data-sources.md §3).

*As built since 2.0.0:* your override is a **Minutes** change (a quick change from a task's sheet, or an instruction Claude reads), the whole task's minutes as you reckon them. Your minutes, and a task of your own's, are planned as you gave them: the learned multiplier corrects the app's estimates, not yours, so it isn't laid over them (`Calibration.multiplierFor`). A task with steps has them scaled to add up to your minutes (`TaskItem.stepScale`). A task of your own (Q28) is planned like any: its *can't start before* is a start the work is planned from (`notBefore`), not a date it's hidden till; one whose steps are all done is finished.

## 3. Day-bucket allocation

The planner builds one bucket per study day from today to the furthest deadline, each with its capacity in minutes, then places every task's remaining work into buckets. **Since review v2 (PL-01), today's bucket is a fair share of every task's days**, and it alone is what the block policy reads (§4).

Placement, tasks taken in deadline order (earliest first; overdue and no-deadline tasks handled below):

1. The last usable day is `dueDay − margin` (margin **1 day** by default, longer for kinds you tend to leave late, §5). If `dueAt` is in the morning (before 12:00) the due day itself is never usable.
2. The task is cut into chunks: its sub-steps if it has them, otherwise boxes of `boxMin` (**ASSUMED 45**, Q6; 30 since review v2's PL-12), last one shorter.
3. **Spread over its window** (review v2, PL-01; until then, backwards from the last usable day). Work with a deadline that isn't due by tomorrow, its last usable day first, is spread from the first day it can be done to its last usable day:
   1. Each piece goes to the day where its share of the window's free time falls: six pieces over twelve even evenings are a piece every second day, not the last six evenings; a day with a busy evening takes a smaller share.
   2. In order: never before the piece before it, and never on a day past which the pieces after it can't follow (a big last piece only a weekend holds keeps the ones before it from passing that weekend).
   3. Over a window of a week or more, within a week of the piece before, where a day in that week has room (the minimum touch, Q-32), so a far task moves on every week.
   4. Revision, or work for a test, a day after the piece before where a later day has room (spaced practice).
   5. A single piece goes to the day with most room, the earlier of two.
   6. A day without room passes its piece to the nearest that has some, earlier first.
   7. What its window can't hold in order at all is placed backwards from the last usable day, as all dated work once was; a chunk that cannot fit anywhere before the deadline is placed in today's bucket and flagged `behind`.
4. Overdue tasks and tasks due today go straight into today's bucket, oldest deadline first; they are placed before step 3 runs so they consume today's capacity first. **Changed 9 Oct (your call): work due later today or tomorrow comes before overdue work**, as its deadlines can still be met: it's placed first, taking today's time before anything already past its deadline, and a task with some brings its own overdue blocks along, ahead of it in their order, ranked by the task's soonest deadline to come rather than their own long gone (so an email's September reply, brought along by tomorrow's application, doesn't go ahead of homework due tonight); every other block ranks by its own deadline, so a task's block due tomorrow doesn't go ahead of other work due tonight either. Then overdue work, oldest deadline first; then the rest as in step 3.
5. The **daily Anki quota** (all due reviews + 20 new from the lowest unfinished deck + any deck named by German homework) is a fixed chunk in today's bucket, deadline 21:00 (**ASSUMED**; could be 22:00 given the weekday window). Homework-linked deck tasks are ordinary tasks with the assignment's deadline.
6. Emails that are just emails (only to read, or about an event; 9 Oct) aren't planned at all, nor reminded of. Nor is a task you've said isn't one, or one waiting, as you said, for another still open; and a day you've limited holds no more work than you said (1.5.0, instructions). Tasks with no deadline (self-sent emails, Power Planner items with no date) get a soft deadline of `firstSeenAt + 7 days` and are placed last, so they fill free capacity rather than displace homework.
7. **As built (Phase 2, 9 Oct):** undated work is planned at most **60 minutes a day** (`softMinPerDay`), working backwards from its soft deadline. On the first day the whole inbox (28 conversations) was first seen at once, so without the cap all of it fell on one evening a week later; with it, it spreads over that week. A task that can only go so far a day (`stepsPerDay`: an Anki deck, which releases 20 new cards a day) never gets more steps than that on one day, overdue or not: an overdue 36-card deck is 20 cards today and 16 tomorrow, not both today. Today's step is what Anki will still show today (with 5 of today's new cards left, 45 unseen cards are 5, 20 and 20), and a deck whose next cards come at 04:00 isn't the thing to do until then. When either kind can't all fit before its deadline, it's done in order from the first day it can be, so as much as possible is done by the deadline (45 cards due in two days: 20 and 20 before it, 5 after); what fits nowhere in the horizon is left unplanned rather than breaking a limit. Remaining time is cut into even boxes (100 minutes is 34 + 33 + 33, not 45 + 45 + 10).
8. **1.7.0 (10 Oct, from the repository review):**
   - A task with steps takes off what its source says is done, as one without does: a 60-minute Power Planner item at 50 %, in two 30-minute blocks, is 30 minutes, not 60. The smaller of the source's progress and the minutes worked counts, so the same work isn't taken off twice.
   - An Anki deck's steps come from its counts, which already leave out the cards studied; the minutes that studied them (`stepsWorkedMin`) aren't taken off the next cards again. Before, 45 cards (9 + 9 + 3 minutes) with the first 20 studied in a 9-minute session planned 8 minutes for the 25 left, not 12.
   - Where your instructions limit days, a box is never longer than the most any day the task can go on holds: an hour due in ten days, with every day limited to 20 minutes, is three pieces of 20, not two of 30 that no day holds (and so nothing planned). Steps aren't cut: a step longer than every limited day still goes unplanned.
9. **1.8.0 (11 Oct): what's left out is said** (`Plan.unplanned`, IMP-P1-002). Open work that isn't in any bucket is listed with its reason, folded under *Not planned* at the foot of the Plan: it can't be started yet (its source's `availableFrom`, or Claude's reading), it waits, as you said, for another task still open, it (or a run of its blocks) opens after the plan's last day, or there's no room for it by that day within each day's limits (a deck past its 90 days of steps, undated work past its daily allowance). A task's runs left out for the same reason are one line. Hidden tasks (only to read, or not a task, as you said), events and Anki's own tasks aren't listed; their reasons are elsewhere. The list only explains: it changes neither the buckets nor blocking.
10. **Review v2 (PL-07): every piece says why it's on its day** (`Chunk.why`), set as it's placed: due today or tomorrow; overdue (since when); spread over the days to its deadline; kept within a week of its last piece; a day apart from the last revision piece; the day with most room (a task in one piece); undated, in spare time this week; packed, as late as its days allow (its window couldn't hold it in order); or couldn't fit before its deadline (`behind` with no room before the deadline itself, whatever placed it, but overdue and undated work, which say that instead; behind only the learned safety margin, a piece keeps the reason it was placed for); since phase 3, where you put it (a day or a time you gave, PL-04), or moved when its time passed (its slot missed, PL-13). The piece's sheet says it in a sentence; the plan Claude reads in the chat has it as a short code (`[why: spread]`), with the codes explained once.
11. **Review v2 (PL-14): dates the app or Claude set are plans; only a source's, the student's, or an email's quoted deadline can be overdue.** A deadline is real (`TaskItem.hardDeadline`) when it's Teams', Power Planner's, Anki's, one you gave (an instruction, or a task of your own), or an email's own words as Claude quoted them (`deadlineQuoted`, AI-12). A date is real only when it's the one in force: a test date Claude read into an assignment before its source's deadline is a plan, and once it passes the source's own deadline holds. Everything else orders the work but is never overdue, never red, and moves on once it passes: a date Claude read into an email without quoting it, or the rules' guess, is a plan, and once gone by the task is undated again, its week counted on from that date; a step's own dates gone by are dropped, so the step is done with its task's own deadline; undated work's soft week, ended undone, is followed by the next (from when it was first seen, a week at a time, not a day at a time, so it still comes round by its week's end rather than always sitting a week away). Tasks, the task sheet, the chat's task list and each completion's record go by the deadline in force (`TaskItem.deadlineAt`): a plan's date gone by is under *Today*, not *Overdue*, or under the real deadline's group where one is behind it, and the sheet says it's planned again; a reading of an email that its new content has made out of date quotes nothing; Stats' "on time", and the margins and box lengths learned from finishing in time (§5), count real deadlines only (`CompletionRecord.hardDeadline`, true for records from before): a box session on work done by a plan's date, or none, counts for no box, for or against.
12. **Review v2 (PL-02): a real deadline its days can't hold is said, the day it becomes so.** When a task with a real deadline still to come has more left than the free time before that deadline (at its turn, before it takes any), or some of it fits nowhere or is carried past the deadline (a per-day limit: a deck's cards a day), the plan says so once (`Plan.wontFit`; a task in runs, for all its runs with that deadline against the time free before the first; never a deadline past the plan's reach, whose later days weren't counted): under today's heading, "6 h left, 4 h free before Sat 09:00", with *What gives?*, which asks Claude in the chat what to shorten, move or ask more time for. The free time runs to the deadline itself (its day, or the day before for a morning deadline), as much of each day as the task can have (on a day it can only start late, the time after its start), not to its last usable day: the safety margin is the app's own, and work inside it, flagged behind, may still be done in time. Overdue work isn't said: its deadline has already gone. Undated work and the app's own dates never are.


Why spread rather than backwards (review v2, your note 3): placing backwards made today's bucket exactly what had to happen today for every deadline to be met, but a far task got nothing until its last days, then arrived all at once with everything else. Spreading gives every day a share of each task's window in proportion to the time it has, so the work moves on every week. Placing everything as early as possible would still put every open task into today and the block would never lift; spreading doesn't, because today holds a share of the days to each deadline, not all of them, and the block reads today's bucket alone (§4), so it lifts when today's share is done.

**Within a bucket**, order is (since 9 Oct): due later today or tomorrow (a task's overdue blocks before its own due-soon ones) → overdue → `behind` chunks → ascending deadline → kind weight (Homework > Revision > Admin > Event prep) → shorter first. Ties broken by title so the widget never flickers.

13. **Review v2 (PL-15): a school day's time is windows.** Its study periods (the timetable's lessons named *Study Period*, `Settings.studyPeriodName`), break (10:45–11:15), lunch either side of eating (13:15–13:40 and 13:55–14:35) and the evening; a weekend or a day with no school (the school calendar, below) is its hours as one window, and a half-day's school windows end when it does, its evening starting then (`core/SchoolDays.kt`). A day's capacity is the sum, a stretch of a school window with under 15 minutes free (`minSchoolSlotMin`) left out, as the slotter lays nothing in one; time taken at no set time comes off the evening first; the evening's part is multiplied by the day's kind's yield and school's by school's (PL-06). Each piece says where it can be done (`Chunk.where`): Teams homework at home (the laptop, the books), unless Claude reads a step as one to do anywhere (the Assignment job's `where`); your own tasks as you set them; the rest anywhere. A piece for home takes only the evening's time; one done anywhere takes school's first and the evening's only past it, so school time goes to what can use it. A task stays one item with each piece taking its own time, so its order, its week, its revision gap and a per-day limit hold across its pieces whatever their kinds. With no margin, a deadline's own day is usable where a window the work can use comes before the deadline (break before a 13:00 one, for work done anywhere; only the evening, for work for home), and a real deadline's work has only that day's time before it there, as its slot must end by then; what won't fit counts the same. Without windows (as in most tests) a day is your hours, as before.
14. **Review v2 (PL-12): every piece gets a time.** On a day with windows its pieces are laid out in them, in the day's order (`core/Slotter.kt`, `DayBucket.slots`): each at the first free time it fits whole, around busy time, with five minutes (`slotBreakMin`) before and after it in its window, and after it a quarter of an hour (`slotLongBreakMin`) once an hour and a half (`slotLongBreakAfterMin`) of work has gone without a long break; work for home only in the evening; a piece due that day at a real deadline only in a slot over by then; none before its task opens; today's from now, on five minutes. First come breaks outside on a weekend or a day off: three (`outsideBreaks`) of 20 minutes (`outsideBreakMin`), at or after 09:30, 13:00 and 16:00 (`outsideTimes`), each over by the next one's time, so on a day under way one whose time has gone isn't laid after now; when the day's work needs their time (with its rests, the long ones too, as they'd be laid) the midday one is given up first, then the afternoon's, then the morning's, and fewer still while, laid around them, more of the work would be left unslotted than with none. Then pins (PL-04): each at its time where that's free and its piece may be done then (a window for its kind of work, over by its deadline, its task open), one under way kept; the others at the next free start, so a pin moved never takes another's time. Then today's starts from the last plan (PL-13), kept where still free, one under way too: by each piece's key, its task and its step's place (a piece of time with no step, its part), so ticking one piece doesn't renumber the next and move it. A piece no free time holds whole is unslotted, still the day's. The next thing to do is the slot under way, else today's next slot, else today's work with no slot (today's comes before tomorrow's), else tomorrow's first slot, else as before; the Plan tab shows each piece's time ("17:00–17:30"). Pieces are cut into 30 minutes (`boxMin`, was 45).
15. **Review v2 (PL-04): a day for all of a task, a time for its next piece.** Told to the chat or as an instruction ("all of the essay on Thursday", "put the physics at 19:30"), they're changes (`OnDay`, `AtTime`) laid on the task (`userOnDay`, `userAtTime`). A day: all of the task's work on it, that day its last usable day whatever the margin, still a deadline only where the task's own is that day. A time: its next piece a run of its own on that day, from the time, which the slotter lays it at; the rest after it. Work you put on a day is placed before everything else, so it has the day's time; it says *Where you put it*. A day or a time after the task's real deadline isn't applied ("It's due before then"); one whose day has gone, or before the task can start, isn't kept to; a time past a plan's date leaves that date behind, the work undated from the time, its week counted from it. A later day for all of it drops a time on another day, and keeps one on that day, whichever was given first.
16. **Review v2 (PL-13): the plan follows the day as it goes.** It's made again whenever what it depends on changes (the tasks, the settings, your instructions, the calendar, the school calendar, today's slots, the calibration), once they've been quiet two seconds (`PlanWatcher`), and each new plan's slot starts are kept (`RuntimeState.slots`), so the next plan lays today's pieces where they were wherever that's still free: a replan doesn't shuffle the day. An alarm rings at the next slot's start and at the next slot's end ten minutes on (`SlotAlarms`), set again with every plan. A slot just over stays where it was those ten minutes (`Slotter.GRACE_MS`), its piece still to tick; then, its piece undone, it's missed (`SlotState.missed`, kept for the day): its start is no longer kept, so the plan moves it to the next free time, and whether that's tomorrow is noted (`spilled`). One whose alarm couldn't ring (the phone off) is found at the next start or the focus service's next look. A time, a day or a start you then give the task forgets its missed slots, as moving it was on purpose.

**The school calendar** (review v2, PL-16, `core/SchoolCalendar.kt`, `school.json`): days with no school (a holiday, half term, an INSET day) and half-days, told to the chat ("half term is 26 to 30 October" is one change over the stretch) or added in Settings → School, where each is deleted again. Stretches that overlap or run on from each other are one. Phase 3's windows read it: a day off is planned as a weekend is, and a half-day's school ends when it does. Backups keep it (format 3; an older backup is read with none).

## 4. Block policy

```
today    = today's bucket (chunks, not whole tasks)
pressure = today is non-empty            ──since review v2 (PL-01): later work is spread, so
                                           tomorrow always holds some; before, today ∪ tomorrow
quiet    = now in [22:30, 07:00)        ──your sleep window (7 Oct)

shouldBlock(now) = !quiet && ( pressure || credit <= 0 )
```

- **While `pressure`:** strict. No credit can be spent, none is earned beyond being banked. Blocked apps show the block screen with the top chunk of today's bucket.
- **When `pressure` is empty:** earned free time. Each verified completion banks `min(30, max(10, remainingMin / 2))` minutes (whole task) or `boxMin / 3` (one chunk). Credit is spent only while a blocked app is in the foreground. Unspent credit expires at the end of the study day. Since 1.8.0, while it's spent a silent notification counts it down, and a heads-up comes a minute before the end (IMP-P2-011); credit earned meanwhile (work a sync finds done) moves both to the new end; neither grants time, and *Back to work* opens the Plan. With no credit, the block screen shows the next chunk from the nearest future bucket as the thing to do to earn time.
- **Quiet hours** are the only unconditional release, so lengthening them (any change that adds time to them) is a "loosening" change subject to the 24-hour delay (§6), and shortening them, which blocks more, applies at once (as built, `SettingsChanges`; this line said the reverse until the 10 Oct review).
- **No bypass button.** Removed at your request. The only override is the parent-held code (§6).

**As built (Phase 3, 9 Oct).** The policy (`block/BlockPolicy.kt`) applies, in order:
- a focus session you started blocked whatever the hour (until review v2: AR-09 removed sessions);
- a parent-approved unblock lets everything through for an hour;
- quiet hours (22:30–07:00) and, on weekdays, school hours (before 16:45, Q6b) never block;
- then pressure blocks strictly, and with no pressure earned time is spent.

**Review v2 (BL-01, BL-02, BL-11): what blocks.** Blocked apps are blocked while today's work isn't done: today's slots have pieces still to do, or a slot missed today went to tomorrow, until the day's hours end (`Reason.TodayUndone`); tomorrow's work alone doesn't block, and a slot you move yourself (a time, a day or a start you give its task) is forgotten, so moving it doesn't keep tonight blocked the way letting it lapse does. Blocking starts at 16:45 on a school day (`weekdayBlockFromMin`; before it the phone at school is free, BL-09) and at 10:00 on a weekend or a day off school (`weekendBlockFromMin`, by the school calendar), outside the quiet hours (`Reason.BeforeBlock`). The block screen says which: a missed slot gone to tomorrow, or today's pieces.

Earned time: a finished focus session earned a third of its minutes (Q22; since review v2 a kept slot does, BL-08 below). A task its source confirms done earns a third of what no session (since review v2, no kept slot) counted, up to 30 minutes, with **no floor** and nothing for reading or archiving an email or an event passing: with the 10-minute floor, archiving a pile of emails would have earned time. *Since 2.0.0:* a task of your own you say is done (*Done*, Q28) earns nothing, as nothing but you confirms it; its sessions (now its kept slots) earn theirs. And a completion earns by your minutes or the app's estimate, whichever is less (`Focus.onCompleted`): raising a task's minutes plans more time for it, but can't make finishing it earn more. Blocked apps and sites are in `block/Blocklist.kt` (Q8). Chrome's and Brave's address bars are read (`<browser>:id/url_bar`), so a blocked site is covered as soon as its address is typed, before it loads; Firefox and Tor are covered outright.

**Review v2 (BL-08): what earns free time.** A kept slot (§4's third way below) earns a third of its piece's minutes (`workMinPerFreeMin`) on the tick when the piece is under 20 minutes (`photoFromMin`); a longer piece's earns when its photo check passes, once, or on the photo itself when it comes before the tick. A longer piece no photo can check (work that isn't written: revision, admin, a deck) earns by its source instead: AnkiDroid's cards counted as you leave it, or the source saying the task's done the same day; one of your own that isn't written work has nothing to check it, so it earns nothing, and the line and *Done* say so. A slot missed today and done tonight is today's, wherever the plan has moved it. Keeping a slot, or a photo's earning for one kept already, is saved before anything changes, so a stop part-way is carried through at the next start, once, before an empty day is judged. A photo is matched to the slot it shows by when it was taken: a slot of its key kept since (ticked while the check ran) earns by it; one kept before it was taken (an earlier box, a round before the task reopened) doesn't, and the piece in the plan now is kept. What today's slots earn is at most today's plan's share: its pieces left and those kept today, at the ratio. So a piece ticked ahead of its day, or work past the day's plan, earns nothing more today; a completion's free time stands beside that share, not in it. Raising `photoFromMin` lets more pieces earn on a tick, so it's a loosening change (§6).

**Review v2 (BL-04, BL-07): the reward in sight, and a day with nothing to do.** Where the block meets you (under the block screen's headline, on the Plan's *Do now* card, on the widget) a line says what finishing today earns: "Finish today's 3 slots (1 h) → 10 min free, 10 min more with a photo of Statics Prep", the short pieces' share on their ticks and the longer ones' with a photo (or "once Teams says … is done", for one no photo can check); once today's done, "Today's plan is done. 25 min free." Between today's slots, after one kept, earned time can be spent till the next slot starts (BL-10), and its countdown ends then: a slot under way, or one gone by undone, closes the gap, and one missed since the keep opens no other at its new time. A day with nothing to do starts with an hour of free time (`freeAllowanceMin`), given once: nothing planned today or overdue, no slot of today's kept, missed or moved, the morning's record planning nothing, and each source read that day (one never read, not set up, aside), so a list from before the day's first sync can't give it. A day emptied by finishing its work has what it earned, and one emptied by moving its work away what you chose ("Nothing planned today: 1 h free." only on a day that had nothing).

**Chunk completion.** The final state of a task is always source-verified (hand in, tick, archive, deck count). A *chunk* of a bigger task cannot be verified at the source, so a chunk completes in one of these ways:

1. **Focus session** (until review v2). Tapping **Start** ran a timer for the chunk's minutes, blocked apps blocked throughout, and the chunk was done when it ended; a session ticked the step it was started on, by its place in the task's list, and its countdown went by the uptime clock that ended it. *Removed in review v2 (AR-09):* you didn't use them; a piece is done by its tick (3, below) or its photo (2), and a session's records in an old `runtime.json` or `log.json` are passed over as they load.
2. **Photo check** (Phase 5, optional). For written work you photograph the pages; the LLM (vision) answers "does this show completed answers for Ch17 questions 1–8?" with a yes/no and a confidence; yes marks the chunk done. Costs cents per check, well inside your credit. Since review v2 (BL-08) a photo of a piece keeps its slot, or earns for one kept already.
3. **Done (review v2, LR-09).** Ticking a piece *Done* keeps its slot (`Focus.keepSlot`): its step ticked, or for a box its minutes added to the task's worked minutes, and a `SlotRecord` in the log with how it went: kept, kept late (after its slot's grace, or missed first: then it's that slot, where it was laid), missed (its end and grace gone by undone), or moved (by a time, a day or a start you gave). A tick only keeps a slot once: a second tap, or a sheet left open on a box whose next piece has the same key, keeps nothing. One of your own whose minutes a tick finishes is finished, as *Done* does.

## 5. Learning and calibration

Everything the app learns is a number you can see in Settings, and every parameter has bounds. The record per task: estimate, actual minutes (sum of sessions until verified done; since review v2's LR-09, of kept slots), kind, class, hour each session started, blocks triggered before the first session ("resistance"), whether the deadline was met, how many days before the deadline it was finished.

1. **Effort multipliers**, per (kind, class): exponentially weighted mean of `actual / estimate`, clamp 0.5–3.0. Applied in §2. Physics preps taking 1.6× the LLM's guess is the kind of thing this catches in a week. *Since 2.0.0* a completion planned by your own minutes (`CompletionRecord.yourEstimate`) teaches it nothing: the multiplier corrects the app's estimates, which yours aren't.
2. **Safety margin**, per kind: starts at 1 day; rises by 1 (max 3) when two tasks of that kind in a row finished within an hour of the deadline or missed it; falls back when five in a row finished early. Pulls chunks earlier for the kinds you leave late.
3. **Box length**, per kind: an experiment over {25, 45, 60} minutes, epsilon-greedy with reward "session completed and the task's verified completion came within its deadline"; locks in after 20 samples, re-opens if completion rate drops. *Removed in review v2 (LR-09):* its reward was sessions, which are gone; pieces are `boxMin` (30) long.
4. **Capacity reality check.** If today's bucket is completed in full on fewer than 40 % of days over two weeks, the template capacity is overstated; the app proposes lowering it (you confirm) because the alternative is a plan that is always `behind`.
5. **Quick self-assessment after each verified completion** (your choice, 7 Oct): one tap, *harder / as expected / easier*, plus an optional one-line note. Feeds item 1 directly (a "harder" vote nudges the multiplier up even before the actual-minutes signal settles) and is included verbatim in the weekly review.
6. **Weekly check-in questionnaire** (your choice): five fixed questions on Sunday evening before the review runs, each a 1–5 scale or one line: how the week felt, what you avoided and why, what got in the way, what you'd change about the plan, energy by time of day. Answers are stored with the week's log.

## 6. Anti-tamper

Nothing on an un-rooted personal phone is unbypassable: adb from a PC, booting to safe mode, a factory reset, or a second user profile all get round any app. What the layers below do is remove every *impulsive* route, so defeating the block takes a deliberate, slow, visible act.

| Layer | Mechanism | Stops |
|---|---|---|
| 1. No bypass | No override button; quiet hours are the only release | The "just this once" tap |
| 2. Settings guard | The accessibility service watches `com.android.settings`, Samsung Device Care (`com.samsung.android.lool`) and the package installer. When a screen in those apps shows the app's own accessibility toggle, its App info page (Force stop / Uninstall / Clear data), the device-admin deactivation screen, "Reset accessibility settings" / "Reset all settings", or a tap on *Decrastination focus* in an accessibility-shortcut chooser, it presses Back (and Home if Back fails). Proved on the phone in Phase 0 for the service's page, App info and the uninstall prompt (`docs/phase0-findings.md` §5) | Turning the service off, force-stopping, uninstalling, clearing data, resetting settings, putting the service on a key shortcut |
| 3. Device admin | A `DeviceAdminReceiver` with no policies; Android refuses to uninstall an active admin until it is deactivated, and the deactivation screen is behind layer 2 | Uninstall from the launcher or Play Store |
| 4. Delayed loosening | Any change that reduces blocking (longer quiet hours, removing a blocklist entry, lower quota, bigger box, disabling a layer) takes effect **24 hours** after you request it and is shown as pending; tightening is immediate; the delay itself can only be lengthened immediately. The parent-held code below is the one way to skip the wait. *Since 2.0.0 (12 Oct, your call), only the blocker's own settings wait: what's blocked, quiet hours, when school-day blocking starts, the minutes of work for one of free time, protection, and the delay itself. The work (due dates, minutes, working hours, box length, margins, undated work, Anki, Teams syncs, Claude) changes at once; see* As built | Rewriting the rules in a weak moment |
| 5. Watchdog | A periodic job and a boot receiver check the service is enabled, the admin active, and the service on no accessibility shortcut (`accessibility_shortcut_target_service`, `accessibility_button_targets`); if not: a persistent notification, the widget shows "PROTECTION OFF" in red, and the event is logged with its time. With `WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS`, granted once over adb at setup (Q19, decided 8 Oct), it puts the service back and clears the shortcut itself | Quietly leaving it off |
| 6. Parent-held override (your choice, 7 Oct; **as built, an authenticator app's code, no email**: see *As built* below) | As planned: every pending loosening request, and any "protection off" state lasting over an hour, is emailed to **richard.white@lshtm.ac.uk**. The email carries a one-time code; typing it into the app applies that one pending change immediately (or, for a protection-off alert, simply tells him). Design below | Leaving it off for days; being stuck when a change is genuinely needed |

**Parent-held override, how the code works (as planned on 7 Oct; not built: on 9 Oct you chose an authenticator app instead, Q17, so nothing is emailed and a code isn't tied to one request; *As built* below is what the app does).**

- A request (say "remove Instagram from the blocklist") gets a random `requestId`. The code is `HMAC-SHA256(secret, requestId)` truncated to 8 digits; the `secret` is generated at setup, lives only in EncryptedSharedPreferences, and is never shown. One code opens exactly one request; it expires when the 24-hour delay would have elapsed anyway; three wrong entries lock code entry for an hour.
- An "unblock for 60 minutes" request is also allowed through this path, so a genuine emergency has a route that involves another person rather than a button.
- **Delivery must not pass through a mailbox you can read.** If the app sent the code from your own Gmail, it would sit in your Sent folder and the override would be yours, not his. So the sender is a **dedicated mailbox whose credentials your dad enters at setup** (a free Gmail address created for the app; its app password goes into the app's encrypted storage through a setup screen he completes, and the password is never displayed afterwards). The app sends over SMTP (`smtp.gmail.com:465`). Alternative if he would rather not run a mailbox: an authenticator app on *his* phone holding a TOTP secret the app shows once as a QR code at setup; the email then just says "Thomas requested X; if you approve, read him the current code"; no secret ever travels by email. Both are implementable; the dedicated mailbox matches what you asked for and is the **default**.
- The weekly note going to him as well is a toggle, **default off**, since you did not ask for it.
- Nothing else is ever emailed to anyone.

**As built (Phase 3, 9 Oct): left unarmed (Q20), with an authenticator app for the override and no email (Q17).**
- *Arm protection* (Setup → *Blocking and protection*) asks for the device admin, then shows a QR code for your dad's authenticator app and checks a code from it (skippable), then arms. Since 1.8.0, arming again keeps the authenticator already set, once he types a code from it (*Set up a new one instead* and *Skip* stay), and *Arm now?* lists whatever of *What's in place* isn't (the service off, self-repair, alerts, battery); it arms all the same.
- **Unarmed**: the guard is off, loosening applies at once, and the watchdog only warns.
- **Armed**: a loosening change waits `loosenDelayHours` (24) and shows under *Waiting*, where *Cancel* drops it (or setting it back does) and a code from his app applies it at once; *Unblock for an hour* takes a code too. The 24 hours are counted on the phone's uptime clock, which setting the date can't move: hours the phone is switched off don't count. A focus session likewise finished, and earned its time, only when the uptime clock agreed its minutes were up (sessions are gone since review v2).
- **Only the blocker waits** (2.0.0, 12 Oct, your call: the app is an organiser first). Armed, a change waits only if it loosens blocking itself: an app, site or browser taken off its list, longer quiet hours, a later start to school-day blocking, fewer minutes of work for one of free time, disarming, or a shorter delay (`SettingsChanges.LOOSER`). Everything about the work applies at once: working hours, box length, margins, undated work, Anki's textbook and deadline, the Teams syncs, Claude (on, its key, its cap) and the reminders; so do instructions and quick changes of due date (no parent code), and a backup's instructions, log, calibration and calendar answers. Moving work off today and tomorrow can now lift the strict mode, but blocked apps still cost earned free time then, and free time is still earned only by timed sessions (kept slots since review v2), photo checks and work its source confirms. A change already waiting on a setting that no longer waits applies at the next count (within half a minute).
- Each code works once, and three wrong ones lock entry for an hour, across restarts (`protect/Totp.kt`, `CodeLock`).
- A code can't say what it was given for: an authenticator shows the same code for every request, so it works on whatever it's typed into. The dialog names what it will do, in full, and says the code is for your dad to type in himself, seeing that; a code read out over the phone for one change could be used for another, or for the hour's unblock.
- The watchdog (`protect/Watchdog.kt`) runs in the focus service every five minutes, as a job every 15, at boot, and the moment the service is switched off. It reads the four shortcut settings defensively: Android 12+ refuses some to apps, and reading `accessibility_qs_targets` threw on the phone and took the service down before that was caught. A setting it can't read is reported as unknown on the protection screen, never as clear; switching the service off from such a shortcut is undone at once once armed.
- The guard (`protect/GuardRules.kt`) adds this app's storage and device-admin pages, Device Care's page for it, the *Date and time* page (setting the date forward would hurry the parent's hour and a session's end; tried on the phone), and the confirmation pages of *Reset all settings* and *Reset accessibility settings*. The reset pages' button text ("Reset settings") is taken from One UI's other reset pages and is unverified.
- A tap on the service's name on a shortcut page is undone, and the watchdog takes it off any shortcut.
- **A saved file that can't be read never disarms** (1.8.0, IMP-P1-003). `armed` is a setting, and an unreadable `settings.json` used to start from the defaults, unarmed, and so take the device admin away as a finished disarm does. Now each store keeps every top-level field of a bad file that still reads on its own (an app update that can't read one field loses only that field), and settings that can't be read at all start armed while the device admin is on (only arming turns it on). The bad file is kept as `<name>.unreadable-<when>` (the newest three, the one just made always among them), and Setup says what happened until you've seen it. That's on record before the bad file is replaced by what was kept of it, so a start cut short in between finds it, and says it, again. Secrets that can't be decrypted are told apart from none saved: the keystore is tried three times, and the file is left where it is, with a copy beside it, so a keystore that comes back reads it all at the next start; each start that finds it says so till it's on record, and not once you've seen it.
- **A browser's address bar that can't be read is a protection problem** (1.8.0, IMP-P1-005): the focus service counts each visit to Chrome or Brave (its own window coming to the front) in which the address was never read; ten running, and the watchdog reports that browser's blocked sites as uncovered, armed or not, until an address is read again, or the browser is blocked outright (at once, though armed its leaving the checked list waits as a loosening does). A visit can rightly show none (a video full screen), so one alone means nothing; another app's window over it (a notification) isn't a visit.
- **A crashed service is restarted, armed or not.** Android leaves a crashed accessibility service switched on but never binds it again until it's switched off and on (it happened on the phone at 00:59 on 9 Oct). The watchdog notices at its next check (within 15 minutes; at once if anything else runs it), allows a minute for it to come back by itself, then switches it off and on, at most every ten minutes, so a service that crashes as it starts isn't churned. A second trap showed on the phone: Android keeps the crashed connection bound and hands the restarted service to it as well, and that connection then resets the service's connection id. Android still counts the service bound but delivers it no events, so nothing is blocked while everything looks well. So "running" means the instance's own connection works (checked every half minute), and a restart that leaves it stopped within a minute is tried once more at once: the second, with the service already up, gets a clean connection. Tried on the phone by crashing the app (`adb shell am crash`): the first restart was reset 16 ms after connecting, the retry 1.5 s later held, and YouTube was blocked again.

Residual holes stated plainly: adb (`settings put secure enabled_accessibility_services`, or any UiAutomation tool such as `uiautomator`, which pauses every accessibility service while it runs), safe mode, factory reset, a second user profile (the phone already has one, user 150, most likely Secure Folder), and an Android update that changes the Settings screens layer 2 recognises (same class of fragility as the Teams scraper; the guard matches on the app's own name, which is stable, and the setup screen has a "test the guard" button). The override's strength rests on your dad typing his code in himself, seeing what it's for: an authenticator's code can't name the change it approves, and v1 sends nothing to him that could.

Layer 2 needs care so it never traps you out of Settings entirely: it acts only on screens that name this app or the reset pages, never on Settings as a whole, and only once protection is armed (as built: the guard checks `armed`, not the setup checklist).

## 7. Edge cases the tests must cover

- Teams sync fails or is stale: tasks keep their last state; the widget shows "Teams synced 06:42".
- A deadline passes while the widget is on screen: redraw at the deadline (alarm).
- A split-screen or pop-up window holding a blocked app: the service checks every window, not only the active one.
- A blocked app in picture-in-picture: the block screen is started with `FLAG_ACTIVITY_NO_USER_ACTION`, so covering an app never sends it there; one already there is found through the window list (a pinned window is never the one in use, so the active-window check alone misses it), relaunched to full screen (One UI's floating window offers no dismiss action) and covered. Proved in Phase 0 after a blocking test left a YouTube Short floating for 20 minutes (`docs/phase0-findings.md` §5).
- A blocked site in Chrome or Brave whose address is hidden (a video full screen): judged by the page the browser last showed in front while the focus service watched. Nothing is remembered across a restart of the service, as a browser may have moved on unseen, to a blocked page or from one; a browser hiding its address with no page seen since the service connected gets Back once, while anything is blocked, to leave full screen so the address shows (a refused Back is tried again at the next look), and one beside another app is first brought forward, once, to be looked at there. A browser's picture-in-picture video is judged by the page the browser showed as it went into the corner, not by what its other windows show since. One that still hides it is taken as not blocked: Back again and again would make a browser whose address bar can't be read unusable.
- An automatic Teams sync the widget doesn't start (its sync service off, no answer): offered again in 15 minutes, up to twice in a row, without using up the day's first-unlock sync; after that, at its usual time.
- Blocks with dates of their own (Q12, 1.1.0): a task's blocks are placed run by run, each run of blocks sharing dates in its own window, from its `from` to its own deadline (never after the task's), and in order (a later run is placed first, and each run stays between its neighbours; one with no dates of its own follows the run before it); without a real deadline, a run's soft one counts from when it opens; one opening past the plan's reach waits. An email with blocks still to do stays while it's starred; unstarred, it's done, its blocks with it (review v2, DX-03: before, an archived one stayed as a follow-up).
- A task that disappears from its source: counted done. Teams and Gmail say no more than that (an assignment handed in and one deleted both just leave the list; an email unstarred and one deleted both leave the starred conversations), so the planned `GoneFromSource` reason can't be told; an email starred again reopens and starts a new round.
- The daily quota at 23:30 unmet: it is overdue; whether that blocks at that hour is quiet hours' decision.
- Clock and time-zone changes: planner re-runs on the system broadcasts.
- A pending loosening change crossing midnight, a reboot, or a reinstall: pending changes are persisted with their apply-at time.
- The settings guard must not fire on another app that happens to contain the word "Decrastination" in its text (match on the Settings package *and* the app's own component or package name in the node tree).
- Override codes, as built (an authenticator's time-based codes, which can't name a request): each code works once; three wrong entries lock entry for an hour, and the lockout survives a restart; the dialog says in full what the code will do, as the code itself can't. (Planned on 7 Oct and not built, with the emailed codes: a code for one request not opening another, and the dedicated sender mailbox being unreachable degrading to the 24-hour wait.)
