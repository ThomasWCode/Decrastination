# Compliance: the stores, the platforms, the school systems, the model's terms and the law

What a public release has to satisfy, read from the primary sources on 31 Oct 2026 (the date of access for every page below; a page's own date given where it shows one). **Thin** marks a point resting on one or a non-primary source. The consequences for the design are drawn at the end of each section and gathered in [what-changes.md](what-changes.md).

## Google Play and the accessibility service

The policy ([Play Console Help: AccessibilityService API](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10964491); the policy text in [Permissions and APIs that Access Sensitive Information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888170)):

- Only "services that are designed to help people with disabilities access their device" may set `isAccessibilityTool`; since 11 Dec 2025 Play rejects a non-accessibility app that sets it and Play Protect blocks it on devices ([Android Developers Blog, 11 Dec 2025](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/12/enhancing-android-security-stop-malware.html)). Decrastination is not one.
- Everything else files the **declaration form** (the purposes include "App functionality"), shows **prominent in-app disclosure** and takes **affirmative consent**, and documents the use in the listing. Every Android blocker on Play (Freedom, AppBlock, StayFree, BlockSite) does this, so **covering blocked apps and reading a browser's address bar are permitted with the declaration**.
- The API "cannot be used to: change user settings without their permission; **prevent the ability for users to disable or uninstall any app or service**; work around Android built-in platform security controls, privacy controls and notifications; change or leverage the user interface in a way that is deceptive." It can't be requested for "an app that autonomously initiates, plans, and executes actions or decisions"; "deterministic, rule-based automation is not covered by this prohibition." "Apps must use more narrowly scoped APIs and permissions in lieu of the Accessibility API when possible."
- **Precedent**: Boomerang, a parental-control app, was blocked from May to July 2020 under Deceptive Behavior, the accessibility rules and "Elevated Privilege Abuse" for its anti-uninstall feature, and re-approved only once it was removed ([TechCrunch, 4 Aug 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/08/04/parental-control-app-boomerang-repeatedly-blocked-from-play-store-losing-business/)).
- **Direction of travel**: with Android 17's opt-in Advanced Protection mode on, the accessibility API is restricted to verified Accessibility Tools ([Google Security Blog, 1 Oct 2026](https://blog.google/security/android-advanced-protection-updates/)); AppBlock already tells its users to switch the mode off ([AppBlock](https://appblock.app/advanced-protection-may-affect-appblock-blocking-on-android/)). Not a ban; a warning that accessibility-based blocking is on borrowed time, with no Digital Wellbeing API offered in its place.

**Device admin.** Deprecated for enterprise since Android 9, most policies throwing from Android 10 ([Google, 28 Aug 2025](https://developers.google.com/android/work/device-admin-deprecation)). Play's malware policy: "Don't use device admin features to block uninstallation" and "Don't write code that prevents a user's app from being uninstalled" ([Play: Malware](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888380)).

**`QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES`**: a declaration, permitted only where the app's core purpose is to search all apps (device search, antivirus, file managers); not where "a more targeted scoped package visibility declaration" would do ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10158779)). A blocker listing launchable apps can use `<queries>` with an intent filter (this plan's reading, not policy text).

**Calendar**: ordinary runtime permissions with a clear explanation; no declaration. **`SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW`**: a special permission, no declaration; the app doesn't use it.

**Teens.** Play's target-audience groups are 13 to 15 and 16 to 17 among others; for both, "this age group may be considered to include children in some locales", and any group that includes children triggers the Families policy ([Play: Target audience](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9867159)); the Families policy fixes no age ([Play: Families](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)). The ICO treats under-18s as children (§The UK), so a UK app for 16- to 18-year-olds is exposed to a Families classification at Google's discretion. **AI-generated content**: Play's policy covers "text-to-text conversational chatbots" and requires in-app reporting of offensive AI output ([Play: AI-Generated Content](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13985936)).

**Sideloading.** Restricted Settings (Android 13 on; mandatory in Android 15's CDD) block accessibility, notification access, device admin, overlay and usage access for an app installed from a browser, a messaging app or a file manager, until the user allows them per app in App info ([Android Authority, 12 Sep 2024](https://www.androidauthority.com/android-15-restricted-settings-sideloading-3481098/); [Google](https://support.google.com/android/answer/12623953)); session-based installers (stores) are exempt. **Developer verification** enforces from 30 Sep 2026 in four countries and globally in 2027: Full Distribution ($25, identity verified) or Limited Distribution (free, no identity, **at most 20 devices**, "for students, hobbyists"); adb is unaffected ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/developer-verification/guides)). F-Droid says the scheme "would end the project as it exists today" ([F-Droid, 29 Sep 2025](https://f-droid.org/2025/09/29/google-developer-registration-decree.html)).

**What follows for the design**

1. **Layers 2 and 3 of protection can't ship on Play**: the settings guard (pressing Back on the pages that disable the service) is the named prohibited use, and the device admin's anti-uninstall is named in the malware policy. The Boomerang case shows what happens.
2. The blocker itself, the block screen and the address-bar check ship with the declaration, prominent disclosure and consent.
3. Protection on Android becomes **"no silent bypass"**: the student can switch the service off or uninstall in Settings, and the app can't stop them; what it can do is notice at once (the service's disconnect, the half-minute check), record it, and tell the parent account that blocking is off and since when ([what-changes.md](what-changes.md) §Protection; Q141 in [decisions-needed.md](decisions-needed.md)). Freedom's Android product works this way and has three million users. D-45's "no bypass" becomes "no bypass the parent doesn't hear of".
4. `WRITE_SECURE_SETTINGS` self-repair is gone with adb; the watchdog warns and shows the way back.
5. `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES` goes; `<queries>` lists the blockable apps.
6. A sideloaded companion (the Teams widget) hits Limited Distribution's 20-device limit from 2027 unless the owner verifies for Full Distribution, and each of its users must allow restricted settings by hand.
7. The Families policy is a risk to carry, not a bar: no third-party ads SDKs, no analytics on by default, the data-safety form true.
8. A *Report this message* on every message of Felix's, beside the thumbs.

## iOS

Apple's Screen Time API is three frameworks under one entitlement, `com.apple.developer.family-controls`, which the developer must request before submitting ([Apple: FamilyControls](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/familycontrols)); review takes days to months (thin). Authorisation is **`.individual`** (the device's owner, revocable in Settings at any time) or **`.child`** (a parent approves through Family Sharing; the child then **can't delete the app** and can't sign out of iCloud). The picker lets the user choose apps and web domains "without revealing their choices to the app": the app holds opaque tokens and shields them on its own logic and schedule ([ManagedSettings](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/managedsettings); [DeviceActivity](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/deviceactivity)). It can't read other apps' content, learn which apps were picked or read Safari's URLs; shields show text and colour only. Opal ("there is no other way to do this on iPhone", [Opal](https://help.opalapp.com/v3/why-do-you-need-to-grant-screen-time-api-access)) and Jomo use it; one sec uses a Shortcuts automation instead.

A Teams-reading companion is impossible on iOS: the sandbox "isolates apps from each other" and Apple gives no API for reading another app's data ([Apple DTS](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/818261)).

**What follows**: contingent blocking is possible on iOS (the app decides when to shield), the Teams companion isn't and isn't wanted, and **`.child` authorisation is the technical commitment device Play forbids on Android**: Apple itself stops the child deleting the app, and the parent approves. For the product's core promise, iOS is the stronger platform; for the owner's codebase, it's a second client ([stages.md](stages.md) stage 9; [risks.md](risks.md) R5).

## School platforms

**Microsoft Graph Education.** `educationAssignment` carries the name, instructions, due and close times, status, grading, web link, resources, submissions and rubric ([Microsoft Learn, 3 Dec 2025](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/educationassignment)). Listing them delegated needs `EduAssignments.ReadBasic` or `EduAssignments.Read`; personal accounts aren't supported; students see only their own ([Microsoft Learn, 23 Jul 2025](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/educationclass-list-assignments)). The overview: "school IT admins must first grant consent to the permissions requested by the app" ([Microsoft Learn, 31 May 2025](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/education-overview)). Entra's default lets users consent to low-impact permissions, but Microsoft's managed default policy from mid-2025 requires admin consent for non-low-impact third-party permissions in tenants on default settings ([Office365ITPros, 19 Jun 2025](https://office365itpros.com/2025/06/19/app-consent-policy-user-app-consent/)); whether `EduAssignments.*` counts as low-impact isn't stated anywhere found. **The school's consent is the path**, as the widget's README already says.

**Google Classroom.** `courses.courseWork.list` and `studentSubmissions` with `classroom.coursework.me.readonly`; public apps with data scopes "must complete a verification process" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/workspace/classroom/guides/auth)); the scopes appear to be handled as sensitive (verification, no security assessment; thin). Since 23 Oct 2023, Workspace for Education users designated under 18 are **blocked from unconfigured third-party apps until an admin trusts or limits them**, and "the school organization is responsible for obtaining parental consent where required" ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/13288950)). The school's consent again.

**UK platforms.** Satchel One claims "1 in 3 UK secondary schools" (vendor), its API "configured through a Wonde integration", no public documentation ([G-Cloud listing](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/194656651021934)); ClassCharts has no public API; Arbor and Bromcom have partner APIs the school approves per supplier. No independent share figures for secondary schools; a 700-teacher survey across phases has Teams at 55 % and Google Classroom at 38 % ([Schoolzone](http://www.schoolzone.co.uk/insight/teams-or-google-classroom), undated, thin). **Canvas** (REST, OAuth2 or a user token) and **Schoology** (REST, OAuth 1.0) are the American self-service ones.

**Power Planner.** The apps are GPL-3.0, the web app and identity server MIT; the REST models are public, the API undocumented ([GitHub](https://github.com/powerplanner)). The development key the app uses is the web app's; a product would need the author's word.

**What follows**: there is **no API path to a school's work that the student can open alone**; every one goes through the school's admin, which makes the LMS integrations a school-partner tier ([stages.md](stages.md) stage 8). The product's own path has to need no one's permission: notification emails, calendar feeds and the share sheet ([what-changes.md](what-changes.md) §Ingestion). Reading the phone's notifications (a `NotificationListenerService` on Teams', Classroom's and Satchel's own alerts) would be a fourth, needing no scraping of another app's screens; it's a restricted setting for sideloaded apps and its standing under Play's policies wasn't checked here: **to verify in stage 0**.

## Driving another app's screens

Play's Device and Network Abuse policy forbids apps that access "other apps on the device" in an unauthorised manner, that "use a service or API in a manner that violates its terms of service", and that "circumvent Android sandbox protections in order to derive user activity or user identity from other apps" ([Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888379)); the accessibility policy allows deterministic, rule-based automation with disclosure but asks for narrower APIs "when possible", and the Graph API exists. Microsoft's mobile licence terms say the user will not "work around any technical limitations in the software that only allow you to use it in certain ways", and that school use may be governed by the organisation's agreement ([Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/microsoft-software-license-terms-mobile-29a8e70e-bfff-41eb-b524-6f4d0bff6877)); the consumer Services Agreement bars "impermissible scraping" ([Microsoft, effective 30 Sep 2026](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/servicesagreement)). No Microsoft text names accessibility-driven automation; the student's school's acceptable-use policy is the binding instrument.

**What follows**: the Teams widget's method is not expressly forbidden and is not defensible as a product's foundation: Play's "violates its terms" clause makes Microsoft's terms decisive, Microsoft's "work around technical limitations" clause is wide, and the sanctioned path exists. It stays what it is, a personal tool, offered to power users off Play with its terms said plainly, and never the path the product depends on.

## Gmail and the calendar

App passwords need 2-step verification and "may not" be available on "a work, school, or another organization account" ([Google](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/185833)); Google ended password sign-in for less secure apps on Workspace in 2024 and 2025 ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/6260879)). The Gmail API's `gmail.readonly` is a **restricted scope**: verification over several weeks and an annual CASA assessment for apps that "store or transmit restricted scope data on servers" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification)); a phone-only client that never routes mail through the developer's server may escape the assessment (this plan's reading); assessors list $540 to $3 600 a year (thin on currency). Calendar scopes are sensitive, not restricted (thin); Android's `CalendarProvider` needs only the runtime permission.

**What follows**: a **forwarding address per account** on the server (the student sets one Gmail or Outlook filter) needs no Google review and works for Outlook schools; IMAP stays the self-host option; the Gmail API is a later tier if ever.

## The model's terms and prices

Anthropic's usage policy defines a minor as anyone under 18 and requires products serving minors to follow its minor-safety guidelines ([AUP](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)): organisations "should implement" age verification, content moderation and filtering, monitoring and reporting, educational resources and Anthropic's child-safety system prompt where provided; they "must disclose to their users that they are interacting with an AI system rather than a human"; compliance with child-safety and privacy law should be stated on the organisation's website; Anthropic "will periodically audit"; non-compliance can end the account ([Anthropic support, 16 Mar 2026](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9307344)). The Commercial Terms (17 Jun 2025) say the services "are not for consumer use" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)), which cuts against a 16-year-old holding their own API key.

Prices per million tokens on 31 Oct 2026 ([Anthropic pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)): Opus 5.5 $4 in, $20 out, $0.20 cache hit, $8 hour-cache write; Sonnet 5.5 $2, $10, $0.10, $4; Haiku 5.5 $0.10, $0.50, $0.01, $0.20 under 100 000 tokens of prompt; the Batch API half price; web search $10 a thousand. The 4.7-and-later tokeniser "produces approximately 30 % more tokens for the same text". Comparators: OpenAI's gpt-5-nano $0.05 and $0.40, Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite $0.10 and $0.40 ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing); [Google](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). The only published cost to serve a companion is Character.AI's: under a cent an hour of conversation on its own hardware ([Character.AI, 20 Jun 2024](https://blog.character.ai/optimizing-ai-inference-at-character-ai/)). Every consumer AI product found bundles the model in a subscription; none is bring-your-own-key.

**What follows**: the product holds the key, implements the minors' guidelines (age at install, moderation, reporting, disclosure, the website's statement) and expects audits; **bring-your-own-key is for adults and self-hosters only**; the cost model is in [costs-and-pricing.md](costs-and-pricing.md).

## The UK

**The Children's Code** (a statutory code under s.123 of the Data Protection Act 2018): a child is anyone under 18; it applies to any information-society service "likely to be accessed by children", "more probable than not", apps included ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/services-covered-by-this-code/)). Its fifteen standards include the child's best interests, a DPIA, age-appropriate application, transparency, no detrimental use, high-privacy defaults, data minimisation, **profiling off by default** (and the provider responsible for recommendations made from a child's data), **nudge techniques** not used to lead children to weaker privacy or to keep them using the service, and parental controls made visible to the child ([ICO: Profiling](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/12-profiling/)). The age of consent for an information-society service is 13 under Article 8, where consent is the lawful basis ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/children-and-the-uk-gdpr/what-are-the-rules-about-an-iss-and-consent/)). The ICO fined Reddit £14.47m in 2025 over weak age checks ([EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/ico-fines-reddit-1447m-over-childrens-data-failures-and-weak-age-checks)).

**The Online Safety Act.** Ofcom's position: a chatbot is in scope when users can share its outputs with other users, when users make bots for others, **when it searches the web**, or when it can produce pornography; a service where people interact only with the chatbot is outside it ([onlinesafetyact.net](https://www.onlinesafetyact.net/analysis/chatbots-and-the-online-safety-act/); [Pinsent Masons via Manchester Digital](https://www.manchesterdigital.com/post/pinsentmasons/online-safety-act-duties-cover-gen-ai-and-chatbots-ofcom-confirms); Ofcom's own page refused the fetch). In February 2026 the government announced an amendment to bring all chatbots under the Act's illegal-content duties and a consultation on restricting children's use of chatbots ([gov.uk, 16 Feb 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/pm-no-platform-gets-a-free-pass-government-takes-action-to-keep-children-safe-online)); the outcome wasn't checked (thin).

**What follows**: a DPIA before the beta; profiling-driven features (the learned pace, the mood) justified in it or off by default for under-18s; the nudges' design documented against the nudge standard, with the student's own arming as the "compelling reason"; parental involvement visible to the student; a 13+ product with no parental-consent flow below it; **Felix's web search off** for the product (it alone brings the app into the Online Safety Act's scope as a search service); and a watch on the chatbot consultation, whose outcome could restrict the whole category for under-18s.

## The EU and the US

**EU AI Act**: Article 50 requires a system "intended to interact directly with natural persons" to tell them so, from 2 Aug 2026 ([Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)); Article 5 bans "purposefully manipulative or deceptive techniques" and exploiting vulnerabilities "due to their age" where significant harm results or is likely, since 2 Feb 2025 ([Article 5](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/)). Homework nudges don't meet the significant-harm bar on the text; a persuasive persona aimed at minors should be assessed against it in writing. The Digital Omnibus (in force 27 Jul 2026) deferred high-risk duties and left Articles 5 and 50 as they are (thin on one source's disagreement).

**US**: COPPA covers under-13s only, its amended rule in force 23 Jun 2025, compliance by 22 Apr 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)). **California SB 243** (signed 13 Oct 2025, in force 1 Jan 2026): "companion chatbot" operators must disclose the AI, keep a suicide and self-harm protocol, and for a known minor disclose the AI, remind them to take a break **at least every three hours**, and prevent sexually explicit material; a private right of action at $1 000 a violation; annual reports from July 2027. **New York** General Business Law Article 47 (5 Nov 2025): notice at a session's start and every three hours for everyone, a crisis protocol, penalties to $15 000 a day ([Morrison Foerster, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251120-new-york-and-california-enact-landmark-ai)). Both exempt productivity bots; whether a homework coach with a persona is a "companion" is untested. The FTC's 6(b) inquiry of 11 Sep 2025 into companion chatbots and minors is open ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions)).

**What follows**: a UK-first release postpones the American rules but not their design: AI disclosure at the start and after three hours of chat, a crisis protocol, no sexual content, all in the envelope every preset carries ([felix-public.md](felix-public.md)); and the product described, in its terms and its prompts, as what it is, **a planner's voice, not a companion**, which is also the design that keeps it out of the companion laws' definitions.

## The company

Google Play developer accounts and Stripe both need an adult of eighteen or over; a UK company needs a director, an ICO registration (a yearly fee), terms a solicitor has read, and insurance. If the owner is under eighteen a parent holds the accounts and the company until they aren't ([decisions-needed.md](decisions-needed.md) Q148).
